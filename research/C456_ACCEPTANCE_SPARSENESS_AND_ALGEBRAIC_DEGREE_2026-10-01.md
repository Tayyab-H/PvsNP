# C-456 — sparsity forces high algebraic degree, but not superlinear gate work

**Date:** 1 October 2026  
**Question:** Does the forced sparsity of every OPS separator's accepting set, combined with its algebraic degree over `GF(2)`, control shared computation beyond the essential-input floor?  
**Result:** Every separator has algebraic degree `N-O(N^beta log N)`. Its ANF therefore needs at least `Omega(log N)` AND/OR gates by the elementary degree-growth bound. This is far below the existing near-linear total-gate floor. A concrete `N-1`-gate function simultaneously has all `N` variables essential, degree `N`, and support `2^r-1` for any `r=o(N)`, so sparsity, degree, and essential-variable count cannot by themselves force a superlinear bound. This retires that combined statistic, not the full-promise target.

## 1. Promise-forced support bound

Let `F:{0,1}^N->{0,1}` be any valid total separator, with `N=2^n` and OPS thresholds

```text
s1 = N^beta/(c n),       s2 = N^beta,
L1 = {T : CC(T) <= s1},  L2 = {T : CC(T) <= s2}.
```

Correctness gives

```text
L1 subseteq F^{-1}(1) subseteq L2,
```

up to an immaterial one-gate adjustment if the chosen integer threshold puts `CC=s2` in the free middle band. Circuit counting gives

```text
|F^{-1}(1)| <= |L2| <= 2^(O(s2 log(n+s2))) = 2^(O(N^beta log N)).
```

The first inclusion also ensures `F` is not the zero function.

## 2. Derive the algebraic-degree bound

Write the unique multilinear algebraic normal form of `F` over `GF(2)` as `P_F`, and let its degree be `d`. A nonzero multilinear polynomial on `N` variables of degree at most `d` has Hamming weight at least `2^(N-d)`, the minimum distance of the binary Reed–Muller code. For completeness, induct on `N`: write

```text
P(x',x_N) = P0(x') + x_N P1(x'),
```

where `deg(P1)<=d-1`. If `P1=0`, every value of `P0` is duplicated across the last coordinate and the inductive weight bound doubles. If `P1` is nonzero, each assignment with `P1(x')=1` contributes exactly one 1 among the pair `P0(x'), P0(x')+P1(x')`; the inductive weight bound for `P1` gives at least `2^(N-d)` ones. Thus

```text
2^(N-d) <= |F^(-1)(1)| <= 2^(O(N^beta log N)),
d >= N - O(N^beta log N).
```

For every fixed `beta<1`, this is `d=N-o(N)`.

## 3. What circuit degree growth actually charges

In the `AND/OR/NOT` basis, `NOT(x)=1+x` over `GF(2)` and `OR(x,y)=x+y+xy`. NOT does not raise degree. Each AND or OR gate multiplies polynomials and can at most double the largest degree previously available. If a circuit has `a` AND/OR gates, therefore,

```text
deg(P_F) <= 2^a,      a >= log2(deg(P_F)) >= log2 N - O(1).
```

This is a valid bound for arbitrary DAG fan-out, but it only forces logarithmically many nonlinear gates. Combining it with the established essential-input floor `S >= N-O(N^beta log N)` does not yield a superlinear total-gate lower bound. The proof locates why the degree statistic stalls: high degree can be generated by a long product with only linear work.

## 4. Strong counterconstruction to this mechanism

For `1<=r<N`, define

```text
G_(N,r)(x) = (AND_(i=1)^(N-r) x_i) AND (OR_(j=N-r+1)^N x_j).
```

It has exactly `N-1` binary gates: `N-r-1` AND gates for the first block, `r-1` OR gates for the second block, and one final AND. Every input is essential. Its accepting set fixes the first block to all ones and the second block to any nonzero string, so

```text
|G^(-1)(1)| = 2^r - 1.
```

Its ANF has degree `N`, because the OR of the final `r` variables has a nonzero degree-`r` term, multiplied by the degree-`N-r` first-block monomial. Taking `r=Theta(N^beta log N)` matches the same subexponential-in-`N` support scale forced on a valid separator. Thus the triple “nearly full essential support + nearly maximal ANF degree + OPS-scale sparse accepting set” coexists with an `O(N)` shared circuit.

`G_(N,r)` is **not** a Gap-MCSP separator: it does not accept all low-circuit truth tables. It refutes only a proposed lower-bound mechanism based on these coarse invariants; it is not a counterexample to the promise lower bound.

## 5. Paired construction attempt and exact frontier effect

The algebraic analysis gives no near-linear full-promise separator. The valid unconditional upper remains the circuit-description enumerator: test every description of size at most `s1` against all `N` coordinates, at `O(N 2^(O(N^beta)))` gates. The parity, repeated-block equality, sparse parity-check, and simple global-block canaries also retain shared `O(N)` checkers for their structured predicates; none is a full-promise separator.

**Strongest proved statement:** every valid separator has ANF degree at least `N-O(N^beta log N)` and at least `log_2 N-O(1)` AND/OR gates. These are weaker than the existing ordinary total-gate floor `N-O(N^beta log N)-1`. **Frontier:** no change to the OPS common-fixed-`epsilon` target, the exact enumerator upper bound, or native `rho>=N-o(N)`; no P-vs-NP proof.

## 6. Novelty and next use

The Reed–Muller minimum-distance step is standard; the contribution here is its exact application to **every** promise-separator extension and the explicit linear-size hostile calibration. This is a route-kill for the degree/sparsity/essential-input package, not a new general lower-bound theorem. Do not refine the same support or degree statistic. Any algebraic continuation must use information in the actual pattern of `F` on `L1`—not only the weight, degree, or number of essential table coordinates—and must still prove a gate-growth inequality under unrestricted reuse. Recent implicit-MCSP and sampler-hardness work remains governed by the separate representation/cost audit in [C-449](C449_IMPLICIT_MCSP_MATERIALIZATION_TRANSFER_AUDIT_2026-10-01.md); it does not supply that explicit-table endpoint.
