# C-247 — Multi-hole substitution and the splice-entropy budget

Date: 27 September 2026  
Route: O-153, replace certificate counting by simultaneous context/replacement products.  
Status: exact conditional product theorem; no forced-slot theorem or q lower bound.

## 1. Simultaneous substitution law

Let Pi be a finite accepting proof tree. Mark pairwise vertex-disjoint occurrences v_1,...,v_t, where v_j has state i_j. Let K be the seed support of Pi outside the marked subtrees. For each j, choose any finite proof support C_j rooted at i_j.

If

    K union C_1 union ... union C_t

is consistent, simultaneous substitution gives a finite accepting proof with exactly that support. The proof is by induction from the leaves: every unmarked branch is retained, and each hole is filled by its selected proof. In a sound Gap-MCSP closure, the full cylinder of this union lies in SIZE(s2).

This packages repeated one-hole substitution as one Cartesian product of choices, with consistency checked on the entire union.

## 2. Private-coordinate product bound

For each slot j, choose a replacement subfamily A_j of supports rooted at i_j and a private coordinate set D_j. Assume:

1. the sets D_1,...,D_t are pairwise disjoint;
2. with each C in A_j associate a low anchor table w_C in SIZE(s1) that extends C, and require the restrictions w_C|D_j to be distinct over A_j;
3. K and every replacement from A_l with l!=j leave D_j unfixed;
4. every tuple in A_1 x ... x A_t has a consistent union with K.

For each tuple, choose a table extending the total support union and set each D_j to w_{C_j}|D_j. This is possible because C_j is extended by w_C, while the context and all other replacement supports leave D_j free. Fill all remaining free coordinates canonically. The resulting table extends the mixed support; its restriction to each D_j identifies C_j, so different tuples give different tables. Each table is accepted by simultaneous substitution and therefore belongs to SIZE(s2). Hence

    product_j |A_j| <= |SIZE(s2)|,
    sum_j log2 |A_j| <= log2 |SIZE(s2)| = O(s2 log(s2+n)).

This is a quantitative soundness budget for independently replaceable proof occurrences.

## 3. Recovery of the C-234 conditional amplification

Under C-234's block-isolation premise, take one slot per prefix block, D_j equal to that block's m suffix coordinates, and A_j the 2^m possible repeated functions g, with w_C the repeated anchor w_g. Each replacement is block-supported, the outside context and other replacements leave D_j free, and all choices are mutually consistent. Then the product has size

    (2^m)^r = 2^(rm) = 2^N.

But log2 |SIZE(s2)|=O(s2 n)=o(N) at fixed OPS beta<1. The product bound contradicts soundness. This recovers the soundness contradiction in C-234 once its repeated-state pigeonhole supplies these private slots. The pigeonhole step, which derives a q lower bound from block isolation, remains the separate C-234 argument.

The diagonal equality cover evades the premise: its contexts fingerprint the repeated function on the other blocks, so a slot cannot retain all 2^m replacements. The theorem therefore does not force block isolation or independent slots.

## 4. General consistency hypergraph

Without private coordinates, let A_j be replacement choices at disjoint occurrences and let H be the t-partite hypergraph of tuples whose total supports are consistent and whose canonical completion map is injective. The exact conclusion is

    |H| <= |SIZE(s2)|.

The private-coordinate theorem is the complete-product special case H=A_1 x ... x A_t. This suggests a more robust invariant than certificate count: the splice partition function, the number of distinct accepted completions generated by a family of simultaneous substitutions.

## 5. What still fails

The available proof grammar does not yet force a large H:

- one proof can have many repeated state labels but only nested occurrences, leaving no large disjoint set of holes;
- disjoint holes can have heavily overlapping or opposite-rail support constraints, shrinking H;
- the C-245 marked grammar bounds context height but not the number or geometry of simultaneously usable holes;
- raw choice counts give exp(O(q log(N+q))), which overwhelms the exp(o(N)) soundness budget at q around N.

Nor can the entropy budget alone yield a superlinear q bound: a table has only N coordinate bits, and the existing linear number of seed positions can encode linear-scale fingerprints. Any superlinear theorem must charge the *organization* of overlapping fingerprints/compatibility constraints, not their total information alone.

## 6. Next target

Prove a grammar-sensitive lower bound on the splice partition function H, or a dichotomy:

1. the proof grammar exposes many disjoint, high-entropy replacement slots, making |H|>|SIZE(s2)|; or
2. its overlap/conflict structure has enough circuit-description synchronization complexity to force q>N g(N).

The second branch must account for arbitrary nesting and repeated labels, preserve all seed-incidence patterns, and pass the diagonal equality calibration. In parallel, continue the N polylog N / N^(1+o(1)) full-promise cover search.

No superlinear q bound, near-linear cover, or P-vs-NP proof follows from C-247.

## 7. Literature filter

The closest relevant proof-complexity result is Austrin–Risse's SoS lower bound for monotone MCSP on monotone slice functions with a small errorless heuristic circuit. Their proof reduces an expanding XOR-CSP to the circuit-size formula by local Boolean substitutions, then uses the CSP expansion lower bound. This is a useful design motif for a future artificial readout model, but it does not currently transfer: C-247's objects are positive proof/context support families for a semantic promise separator, not SoS refutations of a circuit formula, and no map from a native closure to the required CSP proof system has been established. [Austrin–Risse, CCC 2023](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2023.31).
