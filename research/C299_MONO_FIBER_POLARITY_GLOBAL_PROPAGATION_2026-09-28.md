# C-299 - Mono-fiber polarity propagates and closes the full lifted-consensus route

Date: 28 September 2026

Classification: **PROVED GLOBAL LEMMA / SCOPED NONLINEAR ROUTE-KILL.** For even side size v>=6, any cut-label lifted-consensus map that covers every ODDFACTOR perfect-matching input has one fixed low table below all YES outputs. A one-code decoder then gives a monotone separator for the matching-sunflower YES/NO distributions, forcing superpolynomial AND cost. This closes the C-298 construction for arbitrary nonlinear cut labels, but not arbitrary C-125 maps or witness sidecars that are not part of the lifted-consensus map.

## 1. The mono-fiber characterization

Use C-298's lifted rails D_(j,b). On a perfect matching M, define

    F(M,e) = { [T] : delta(T) intersect M = {e} }.

C-298's exact criterion simplifies further:

    D_(j,b)(M)=1 iff there is an edge e in M
                   such that z_[T](j)=b for every [T] in F(M,e).

That is, a rail is active exactly when one whole singleton-boundary fiber is monochromatic. To see necessity, if a candidate cut T activates b and e is any edge in B_M(T), every cut U with B_M(U)={e} is an oppositely labelled obstruction unless it also has label b. For sufficiency, if F(M,e) is all b, choose the singleton cut T={u} for the left endpoint u of e and take H=M minus {e}; every odd cut compatible with H has matching boundary exactly {e}.

Write S_j(M) subseteq {0,1} for the colors of mono-fibers at coordinate j. A complete low code below D(M) requires S_j(M) nonempty for every j and every perfect matching M.

## 2. Intersection lemma for neighboring matchings

Suppose M and M' have union equal to one alternating cycle through all 2v vertices. For every e in M and f in M', the fibers F(M,e) and F(M',f) intersect.

Index the cycle's edges in cyclic order. Since edges alternate between M and M', e and f have opposite index parity. The vertex interval between them has odd size; its cut crosses exactly e and f in the cycle. Its cut class is therefore in both fibers.

Consequently:

- A mono b-fiber in M intersects every mono fiber in M'. Thus every mono fiber in M' has color b.
- If M has mono fibers of both colors, M' has no mono fiber at all, contradicting the required nonempty S_j(M').

So whenever every matching has at least one mono fiber, each matching has exactly one available polarity, and neighboring matchings with a single alternating cycle have the same polarity.

## 3. Connectivity when v is even

Identify perfect matchings with permutations in S_v. The union of M and M' is one alternating cycle exactly when their relative permutation is a v-cycle. The graph on S_v generated by multiplying by v-cycles is connected for even v>=6:

- the set of v-cycles is closed under conjugation, so the generated subgroup is normal in S_v;
- it contains an odd permutation because v is even;
- it contains a nonidentity even permutation as a product of two distinct v-cycles;
- simplicity of A_v then puts A_v in the subgroup, and the odd generator gives all of S_v.

The neighbor-intersection lemma therefore propagates one polarity across every perfect matching.

**Global polarity theorem.** For every coordinate j there is a bit b_j such that

    S_j(M) = {b_j} for every perfect matching M.

This theorem allows arbitrary Boolean functions T -> z_[T](j); it is not limited to affine labels.

## 4. The fixed low code and its decoder

Let b=(b_1,...,b_N). On every perfect matching M, there is exactly one active rail at each coordinate, so

    D(M)=e(b).

If D is a C-125 map for ODDFACTOR, its YES condition on M supplies a low table w with e(w)<=e(b). Complete one-hot codes are comparable only when equal, so w=b and b belongs to SIZE(s1).

Every bipartite ODDFACTOR YES input contains some spanning odd-degree subgraph, but it need not contain a perfect matching. Therefore monotonicity from perfect-matching inputs alone does not imply that D(G) contains e(b) on every ODDFACTOR YES input. We do not make that claim.

Instead use the exact promise lower-bound distributions from Cavalar et al. Define

    Q(G)=AND over j in [N] of D_(j,b_j)(G).

Then Q accepts every perfect matching, since D(M)=e(b). On every odd-cut NO graph G, C-298 gives D(G)<=e(z_[T]) for a compatible cut T, where z_[T] is high. Since b is low, b != z_[T], so at a differing coordinate Q rejects. Thus Q separates the paper's YES distribution D1 (uniform perfect matchings) from its NO distribution D0 (odd-cut graphs).

The proof of the matching lower bound explicitly states that correctness only on the support of D=D0 union D1 suffices, and that Match and Odd agree there. Hence Q must have monotone circuit size 2^(v^(1/3-o(1))). Q uses the map's a AND gates plus N-1 decoder AND gates. Flattening free OR regions yields ordinary monotone size O((a+N+1)(v^2+a+N)); therefore a is superpolynomial in N when v is chosen even with v=(log N)^K for any fixed K>3. The construction cannot give a useful small-CohEnc transfer.

This argument deliberately uses the promise lower bound, not the false assertion that every ODDFACTOR YES graph contains a perfect matching.

## 5. Scope

C-299 closes the subgraph-lifted-consensus map D for arbitrary nonlinear labels: if it satisfies the YES LowExt condition on all perfect matching inputs, its map cost inherits the superpolynomial matching-distribution separator lower bound. It also proves the global-polarity conjecture O-175 for even v>=6.

It does not cover a more general map F=D OR W where witness rails W can supply missing bits on some matchings while vanishing on NO. In that case D alone need not have a mono-fiber at every coordinate/matching, and the propagation proof cannot be applied as stated. O-176 tracks that sidecar extension. It also does not produce a general CohEnc lower bound, change q=N-o(N), or prove P != NP.
