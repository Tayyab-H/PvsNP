# C-320 — Robust code-splice balls and the state-graph limit

Date: 28 September 2026  
Route: Q181 / C-281 owner masks; strengthens C-112.  
Classification: **QUANTITATIVE SPLICE LEMMA; NO q LOWER BOUND.**

## 1. Robust split lemma

Let `C subseteq {0,1}^N` be a family of K truth tables with minimum Hamming distance d, all of circuit complexity at most `s1`. For a coordinate set `P subseteq [N]` and an ordered distinct pair `(f,g)`, define the splice

```text
h_(f,g,P)[a] = g[a] if a in P, and f[a] otherwise.
```

Let `M(T)=|SIZE(T)|`. If

```text
d > 2 log2 K + log2 M(T),
```

then a uniform random P has probability at most `K^2 M(T) / 2^d` of producing any off-diagonal splice in `SIZE(T)`. For each fixed `(f,g)`, the splice is uniform over the `2^{dist(f,g)}` completions of their common restriction on the disagreement set; at most `M(T)` such completions have circuit size at most T. Union bound over the ordered pairs. In particular, when the displayed inequality is strict, there exists P for which every off-diagonal splice has complexity greater than T. The same P works for all pairs, and the complementary split has the same property because `h_(f,g,[N]\P)=h_(g,f,P)`.

There is a robust radius. If two truth tables differ at at most r coordinates, patching those coordinates with address minterms changes circuit size by at most `c0*n*r`, for an absolute basis-dependent constant `c0`. Therefore, if every `h_(f,g,P)` has complexity greater than T and

```text
T > s2 + c0*n*r,
```

then every mask `mu` with Hamming distance at most r from P gives `h_(f,g,mu)` of complexity greater than `s2`, for every distinct ordered pair. The same holds for masks within r of the complement of P. Otherwise patching `h_(f,g,mu)` into `h_(f,g,P)` would produce a size-at-most-T circuit.

## 2. OPS-scale instantiation

Use the C-112 constant-distance code subfamily of C-80: `K=2^(Omega(s1))`, `d=Omega(N)`, and every codeword lies in `SIZE(s1)`. The exact OPS parameters are

```text
s1 = N^beta/(c*n),    s2=N^beta,    N=2^n.
```

For every fixed `beta<gamma<1`, put `T=floor(N^gamma)`. Circuit counting gives

```text
2 log K + log M(T)
  = O(N^beta/n + N^gamma*n) = o(N) < d
```

for sufficiently large n. Hence a common split P makes every off-diagonal code splice harder than `N^gamma`. Taking

```text
r = floor((N^gamma-s2-1)/(2*c0*n)) = Theta(N^gamma/n)
```

shows that **every** owner mask within this radius of P or its complement makes every off-diagonal splice high. Since `gamma>beta`, this forbidden radius exceeds `s1` by the factor `N^(gamma-beta)`.

This is stronger than merely finding one high splice mask. It gives a large Hamming neighborhood of owner masks forbidden simultaneously for all pairs in the code family.

## 3. Application to C-281-compatible state products

Suppose a context for codeword f and a replacement proof at a reused state for codeword g are compatible. Their owner mask `mu` gives a completion `h_mu` in `SIZE(s2)` by C-281. If `f != g`, the robust lemma therefore forces

```text
dist(mu,P)>r and dist(mu,[N]\P)>r.
```

Thus any compatible off-diagonal state product avoids two large mask balls. This is a real constraint on the products generated by the native game, not merely on a chosen router.

It still does not bound q. The two forbidden balls occupy only a `2^(-N+O(N^gamma))` fraction of all masks. The state graph can in principle generate safe masks outside them, and C-281 provides no lower bound on the number of different masks a reused state must generate. In particular, the robust splice lemma does not force a product to realize masks near P; that missing forcing step is precisely what a state-synchronization argument must supply. C-257 parity and C-258 equality remain counterexamples to any generic reuse-to-splice claim.

## 4. Literature transfer audit

Jukna's approximate Minkowski-circuit framework studies circuits whose generated vectors lie below a target family and approximate every target vector. Its polynomial-design lower bound is a promising model for support-family factorization: [Jukna, *Notes on Monotone Arithmetic Circuits*](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/boolean/minkowski.html). The direct hypothesis does not hold here. A native support rectangle is sound only in the sense that every full-table completion has circuit size at most `s2`; its completion need not belong to the chosen low code. Dropping that distinction would invalidate the code-design argument.

Jukna and Razborov prove strong lower bounds for restricted read-branching programs computing linear-code characteristic functions: [ECCC TR96-037](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/eccc-reports/1996/TR96-037/index.html). The native state game is not such a program: one state seed is an unbounded disjunction of table literals, and each state imposes two obligations. A reduction preserving q has not been established.

## 5. Disposition

C-320 quantitatively strengthens C-112 and identifies what it buys for the current Q181 route: all compatible off-diagonal owner masks must avoid a common radius-`Theta(N^gamma/n)` neighborhood and its complement. It does not show that a small q graph must generate a forbidden mask. Continue Q181 by trying to force mask dispersion from the alternating transition graph or its compatible-support antichains; otherwise this is a code-splice calibration only. The actual lower bound remains `rho_GapMCSP=N-o(N)`.
