# C-300 - Approximation-support audit of the NO-vanishing sidecar attempt

Date: 28 September 2026  
Classification: **WITHDRAWN ROUTE-KILL / CONDITIONAL EXPANSION LEMMA.** The proposed use of C-286's Rao NO error on odd-cut NO inputs is invalid because those are different distributions. The matching-graph expansion argument below is sound if sidecar sparsity is established by some valid method, but C-300 did not establish that premise. C-301 later resolves the broader polynomial-cost ODDFACTOR map question using the correct odd-cut approximation theorem.

> **Correction.** Rao's spread-matching approximation controls its own NO distribution `G(W)` (graphs containing exactly edges incident to a random vertex set W). C-298/C-299's `W` sidecar is known to vanish on the Cavalar odd-cut NO distribution. This does not imply it vanishes on Rao's `G(W)` inputs; in fact, many `G(W)` inputs are ODDFACTOR-YES. Therefore the claimed `O(v^-1/2)` bound on sidecar support in Section 2 does not follow. Do not cite C-300 as a theorem that excludes this architecture.

## 1. Setup and claim

Use bipartite ODDFACTOR on sides of size `v`, with `v` even. Let `D` be the C-298 lift with high label `z_T` on every odd-cut NO extension, and write

```text
F_(j,b)(G) = D_(j,b)(G) OR W_(j,b)(G).
```

Assume every `W_(j,b)` is zero on every odd-cut NO graph. Let `a` be the total number of distinct binary AND gates in the shared DAG for `F`; the OR of all W rails uses at most `a` of them.

**Theorem.** Fix any sufficiently large constant `K>3` and put `v=2 floor((log N)^K/2)`. For all sufficiently large `N`, no such C-125 map can have `a` bounded by any fixed polynomial in `N`. Equivalently, at this source scale, if the factorization and NO-vanishing condition hold, the shared AND cost is superpolynomial in `N`.

The mechanism is: small AND cost makes the sidecar fire on only `O(v^{-1/2})` of perfect matchings; the remaining matchings contain a giant connected component in the alternating-cycle graph; C-299's fiber-intersection lemma fixes one low code throughout that component; its code decoder has almost-perfect agreement on the matching/odd-cut promise distributions and inherits the exponential monotone lower bound.

## 2. Spread amplification bounds the sidecar's YES support

Let

```text
g(G) = OR over j,b of W_(j,b)(G),
p = Pr_{M uniform perfect matching}[g(M)=1].
```

On the odd-cut distribution `D0`, `g=0`. C-286's directional approximation is proved on Rao's matching YES/NO distributions, where the NO inputs have form `G(W)`. The sidecar hypothesis here only gives `g=0` on odd-cut NO inputs, so the following inference is **not justified**:

```text
Pr_D1[g=1 and g_tilde=0] <= delta1 := a 2^(-t),
Pr_D0[g=0 and g_tilde=1] <= delta0 := a 4^t epsilon.
```

The invalid step is replacing Rao's `G(W)` NO distribution by the odd-cut distribution. Consequently the claimed inequality `p < theta_M+delta1` is unproved for this architecture.

Set `h=v/4`, `k=v/2`, and `gamma=h/v=1/4` in the matching spread lemma. Its spread parameter is `R=v^2/(8k)=v/4`. With `xi=1/4`, any matching-DNF firing on a `q` fraction of `D1` fires on at least `1-xi=3/4` of `D0` whenever

```text
q >= theta_M := 64 alpha_M (t+2)/v.
```

If an independent argument supplied `g=0` on Rao's `G(W)` distribution and `delta0<3/4`, then one would obtain

```text
p < theta_M + delta1.                              (1)
```

This is where the shared-DAG directional error matters: `a` counts distinct AND nodes once, and there is no factor for the number of W rails.

For explicit asymptotics, take Rao's parameterization from C-286:

```text
u = sqrt(v)/(2 sqrt(2)),  t = eta_M^2 u,  epsilon = exp(-eta_M u),
0 < eta_M <= 1/(2 ln 4).
```

Then

```text
delta0 <= a exp(-eta_M sqrt(v)/(4 sqrt(2))),
delta1 <= a exp(-eta_M^2 ln(2) sqrt(v)/(2 sqrt(2))),
theta_M = 16 sqrt(2) alpha_M eta_M^2 / sqrt(v) + 128 alpha_M/v.
```

For `a<=N^C` with fixed `C` and `v=(log N)^K`, `K>3`, both error terms are `o(1)`. Equation (1) gives

```text
p <= 16 sqrt(2) alpha_M eta_M^2 / sqrt(v) + 128 alpha_M/v + o(1)
  = O(v^(-1/2)).
```

Write `B={M:g(M)=1}` and `U={M:g(M)=0}`. At least a `1-p=1-O(v^{-1/2})` fraction of perfect matchings lie in `U`.

## 3. The good matchings retain one common polarity on a giant component

Form the graph `Gamma_v` whose vertices are the `v!` perfect matchings of `K_(v,v)`, with `M~M'` exactly when their union is one alternating cycle through all `2v` vertices. Identify matchings with permutations in `S_v`. This is the Cayley graph generated by all `v`-cycles; its degree is `d=(v-1)!`.

For completeness, its normalized adjacency spectrum follows from the character ratios on a full cycle. By the Murnaghan-Nakayama rule, irreducible characters vanish on a full cycle except for hook partitions `(v-r,1^r)`. For these hooks, the dimension is `binom(v-1,r)` and the character is `(-1)^r`, so the eigenvalues are `(-1)^r/binom(v-1,r)`. When `v` is even, the trivial eigenvalue is `1`, the sign eigenvalue is `-1`, and the second-largest eigenvalue is `lambda_2=1/(v-1)`. Cheeger's inequality therefore gives edge conductance

```text
h_v >= (1-lambda_2)/2 = (v-2)/(2(v-1)).
```

We use the following elementary deletion consequence. In a `d`-regular graph on `m` vertices with edge conductance at least `h`, deleting `p m` vertices, where `p<h/(1+h)`, leaves a component `C` of size at least

```text
|C| >= (1-p-p/h)m = (1-p(1+1/h))m.                 (2)
```

Proof: if every remaining component had size at most `m/2`, all its boundary edges would go to the deleted set. Summing expansion lower bounds over the components would give `h(1-p)dm <= pdm`, contrary to `p<h/(1+h)`. Hence there is a component larger than `m/2`. Every other component has size at most `m/2`; summing its boundary lower bounds and charging those edges to the deleted vertices shows that the total size outside the largest component is at most `pm/h`. This proves (2).

**Conditional only:** if `p=O(v^{-1/2})` is established, (2) gives a component `C` containing a `1-O(v^{-1/2})` fraction of all perfect matchings.

For each coordinate `j` and good matching `M in U`, `W(M)=0`, so the C-125 YES condition gives a low complete code below `D(M)`. By C-298/C-299, this requires at least one monochromatic singleton-boundary fiber at coordinate `j`. If good matchings `M,M'` are adjacent in `Gamma_v`, every fiber for an edge of `M` intersects every fiber for an edge of `M'`: an odd vertex interval between the two opposite-parity edges supplies a cut in the intersection. Therefore every mono-fiber color in `M` equals every mono-fiber color in `M'`. Since `C` is connected and has more than one vertex, each coordinate has a unique available polarity `b_j` throughout `C`.

Set `b=(b_1,...,b_N)`. On every `M in C`, `D(M)=e(b)`. The C-125 YES condition forces `b in SIZE(s1)`: a complete one-hot code below `e(b)` must equal `e(b)`.

## 4. Conditional decoder consequence

Define the monotone circuit

```text
Q(G) = AND over j in [N] of D_(j,b_j)(G).
```

Under the extra premise that produced the large component, it accepts every matching in `C`, hence a `1-O(v^{-1/2})` fraction of `D1`. On every odd-cut NO input, all W rails vanish and `F=D`; the C-125 NO condition gives `D<=e(z)` for some `z` outside `SIZE(s2)`. Since `b` is low, `b!=z`, so at a differing coordinate `D_(j,b_j)=0`. Thus `Q=0` on the odd-cut distribution.

The equal mixture `D=(D0+D1)/2` therefore has agreement

```text
Pr_D[Q=Odd] >= 1 - O(v^(-1/2)).
```

The matching-sunflower proof gives the same lower bound for a monotone circuit with this distributional agreement, not only for a circuit correct on the entire promise. Indeed, its gatewise approximation step approximates an arbitrary small monotone circuit by a small DNF on `D`, while its DNF lemma bounds agreement with `Match` by `1/2+o(1)`; a circuit agreeing `1-o(1)` with `Odd` on `D` contradicts that bound because `Odd=Match` on the support of `D`. Hence `Q` requires size `2^(v^(1/3-o(1)))`.

Collapsing maximal OR-only regions in a monotone DAG with `a_D` paid AND gates and `N` decoder rails gives an ordinary monotone circuit of size

```text
O((a_D+N+1)(v^2+a_D+N)).
```

Here `a_D<=a`. If `a<=N^C`, this is polynomial in `N`, while for fixed `K>3`, `2^(v^(1/3-o(1)))` dominates every fixed polynomial in `N`. Contradiction.

## 5. What this resolves and what remains

The spectral-expansion deletion lemma is valid, and the conditional reasoning shows how a valid sparse-exceptional-set theorem could preserve C-299 polarity. But the Rao-to-odd-cut distribution substitution needed to instantiate it was not proved. C-300 therefore does not resolve O-176.

The intended factorization is limited to `F=D OR W` with the C-298 lift. The broader arbitrary-map statement is proved separately in C-301 through Cavalar's odd-cut approximation. C-300 itself proves no encoder lower bound.

No near-lossless transfer, superlinear `q`, improved `rho_GapMCSP`, `NP not subset P/poly`, or P-vs-NP proof follows. The actual fusion bound remains `q=N-o(N)`.
