## Exact closure-game formulation

For a proposed semantic pair list \(Q=((E_i,H_i))_{i=1}^q\), let \(T_i=E_i\cap H_i\). For each anchor \(w\), begin with the upward closure of its matching literal slices. Let \(x_i(w)\) mean that rule \(i\)'s consequence \(T_i\) enters the least closure. Its least fixed point is determined by
\[
x_i(w)=\left(a_i(w)\vee\bigvee_{j:T_j\subseteq E_i}x_j(w)\right)
\wedge
\left(b_i(w)\vee\bigvee_{j:T_j\subseteq H_i}x_j(w)\right),
\]
where \(a_i(w)=1\) iff some matching literal slice is contained in \(E_i\), and \(b_i(w)=1\) iff some matching literal slice is contained in \(H_i\). Each seed predicate is a disjunction of signed truth-table bits; a proper endpoint contains at most one polarity per coordinate. Start at the all-zero state and iterate; at most \(q\) state bits flip, so the least fixed point is reached after at most \(q\) strict rounds. The closure derives \(\emptyset\) iff some active rule has \(T_i=\emptyset\). If it does not, the least closure itself is a proper semi-filter above \(w\) preserved by every pair in \(Q\). Hence the central question is exactly whether this cyclic two-premise system can activate an empty rule on every low-circuit anchor.

For the dense OPS high side, every two-coordinate cylinder contains a high table. Thus an empty-consequence rule cannot activate directly from the initial literal slices; a contradiction proof needs a nonempty bootstrap. Unfolding a round-\(t\) proof gives at most \(2^t\) literal leaves, while C-03 forces \(N-o(N)\) distinct coordinates, so \(t\ge\log_2N-o(\log N)\) (C-72). This is a depth bound, not a superlinear rule-count bound.

The raw surviving-anchor count is not a valid potential: one pair can affect \(N\) or more anchors (C-09). The distance \(\delta_Q(w)\) to empty closure is valid per anchor, but C-73 refutes the simple static-weight strategy: for every distribution on low anchors, some literal-pair rule at \(Q=\varnothing\) saves one rule for at least \(1/4-o(1)\) of its mass. The next invariant must charge shared intermediate intersections once, rather than seek tiny additive marginal progress for every pair. Endpoints remain arbitrary semantic sets, so containment relations \(T_j\subseteq E_i,H_i\) cannot be compressed by an endpoint-description argument. Details and the exact obstruction are in [the closure-game report](FUSION_CLOSURE_GAME_2026-09-26.md).

## Artificial gap calibration

Existing exact models isolate two opposite behaviors:

- Shared-core edge filters reduce to graph cuts: for \(K_m\), the integral cover is \(\lceil\log_2m\rceil\), while the fractional cut cover is about 2 (audit Proposition 18.18). This gives a growing but only logarithmic gap, and it does not satisfy the full anchor condition.
- Co-singleton filters have exact cover \(\binom N2\), but every pair covers at most one filter, so the fractional optimum is also quadratic; they miss the linear fractional ceiling and the anchor condition (audit Proposition 18.19).

These are useful controls, not the required artificial model. No model currently meets all of: per-anchor \(\le N-1\), global fractional \(O(N)\), and global integral \(N^{1+\varepsilon}\), while retaining arbitrary semantic pairs and the anchor literals.

## C-90 promise/full-domain distinction

The active cover \(\rho_{\rm prom}\) lives on \(Y\sqcup Z\) with filter universe Z. A separate parameter \(\rho_{\rm full}\) includes the medium band M in its universe. Do not conflate them. The reverse map from a rect-DAG for \(Y\times Z\) to \(\rho_{\rm prom}\) is O(L) via its separator circuit and the set-construction representation. The medium-band escape filter only blocks direct reuse when upgrading to \(\rho_{\rm full}\). A full-domain cover restricts to a promise cover, so \(\rho_{\rm prom}\le\rho_{\rm full}\). Detailed proof and corrected C-77/C-89 interpretation are in [the DAG/Fusion bridge audit](DAG_FUSION_BRIDGE_2026-09-26.md), C-90.

## C-91 - SCC-sensitive unrolling

First delete each rule self-support term: T_i=E_i intersect H_i lies in both endpoints, but these self-loops do not change the least fixed point. In the resulting loop-free support graph, let r_C be SCC sizes and e_C internal side-support incidences. C-91 gives a fan-in-two separator of size O(qN+q^2+sum_C(r_C e_C+r_C^2))=O(q^2+q d^2), where d=max_C r_C and q>=N-o(N). It is O(q^2) for acyclic dependency, sparse SCCs, or d<=sqrt(q). The large dense SCC case remains open.

## C-92/C-93 - Reduce the recursive carrier graph

C-92 is a proved recurrence pruning: after self-loop deletion, strict non-cover carrier supports can be removed from both sides because retained cover edges force every deleted lower-carrier state to imply the upper-carrier state at every fixed point. ## C-94 - Escape supports are the only feedback source

After the C-91/C-92/C-93 reductions, strict subcarrier supports are exactly the shared two-sided Hasse-cover signals. Factoring the shared cover signal gives H_t=K_t OR D_t, where D_t contains the candidate-specific escape conjunctions. The carrier Hasse graph is acyclic, so every directed cycle uses an escape edge. The next target is escape-alternative non-shareability.

## C-98 - Conditional crypto calibration of shared-DAG hardness

## C-99 - The cryptographic benchmark is not an independent separation route

Keep it as a model calibration, not an independent proof route. Uniform PRF security does not rule out arbitrary nonuniform h_n circuits.

## C-385 game-form correspondence update

The exact C-319 recurrence is an alternating reachability graph game: each rule state is universal over its two sides, each side existentially chooses a predecessor or its seed terminal, true terminals win, false terminals lose, and seedless cycles lose. Thus Q has O(q) game vertices, O(q^2) possible edges, and 2q terminal inputs whose values are endpoint-selected ORs of table literals. Pauly's graph-game form framework is the right general language; it proves monotonicity and LFP closure but leaves circuit-size succinctness questions, with no Gap-MCSP-specific lower bound. Our O(q^2) round unrolling is the known upper compiler, not a lower bound. See the addendum in C-385 and [Pauly](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.03093).

## C-421 - Every separator yields an anti-checker certificate, with length at most N

## C-423 - Boundary pivots do not generically recover the maximin sample

For a separator C, choose a Low endpoint g, walk from a rejected reference f to g by random coordinate order, and sample the first coordinate whose flip makes C accept. The hoped-for uniform-endpoint pivot law was a constant anti-checker for every Low table. Sparse parity endpoints give a second calibration with hit probability r/N. This is a counterexample only to a geometry/entropy/separator-value lemma: its rejected f=0 is itself Low for MCSP, so no actual Gap-MCSP separator is constructed. An actual-High-specific coverage theorem remains possible.

The paired full-promise linear-sketch attempt also fails: if H has rank k<N-O(N^beta n), its kernel has more elements than SIZE(<s2), hence contains a High table that collides with Low 0^N. Thus linear fingerprints cannot compress to substantially fewer than N bits; nonlinear separator construction remains open. Exact enumeration stays O(N*2^(O(N^beta))) total gates. The requested parity, repeated-block equality, sparse parity-check, and global block-relation checks all admit shared O(N) or O(L) gate implementations; they invalidate incidence charges but are not full-promise separators.

## C-424 - Actual High table defeats the local pivot law; completeness is the missing global constraint

Choose `alpha` with `beta<alpha<1`, hide a counting-hard `k=floor(alpha n)`-variable function q on an address subcube B of size `m=Theta(N^alpha)`, and set the table f to zero outside B. Restriction gives `CC(f)>=CC(q)=Omega(N^alpha/n)>s2`, so f is OPS-High. With `t=Theta(N^beta/n^2)`, construct `2^t` low tables that are one on a large fixed region A0, zero on B and a marker p, and arbitrary on T, plus the special low singleton h=1_p. An O(N)-gate circuit C accepts exactly these endpoints: one conjunction branch recognizes A0=1,B=0,p=0, and one point clause recognizes h. Every accepted table has a circuit of size O(tn+n)<s1, so C rejects every High table globally. It is sound but incomplete, since it rejects other Low tables such as zero.

On a uniformly random coordinate-order path from f to a generic endpoint, the pivot is the last among `a=|A0|=N-o(N)` mandatory A0 flips and `b=wt(q)<=m` mandatory B flips. For h, the pivot hits its disagreement set with probability at most `b/(a+b)+1/(2^t+1)=O(N^(alpha-1))+2^-t=o(1)`. Thus actual High hardness and soundness over every High input still do not give pivot coverage; completeness on the entire Low class is the omitted resource. The construction is not a full-promise separator and changes no lower bound.

A certificate-cover attempt gives another exact but weak constraint: every accepting certificate cube lies within `SIZE(<s2)`, so it has at most `O(N^beta n)` free coordinates. A Hamming-separated Low code of size `2^(Theta(N^beta/n^2))` therefore needs that many distinct certificates, but counting partial assignments over the at most `S+1` essential inputs yields only `S=Omega(N^beta/n^2)`. This is below the existing `N-o(N)` floor; raw certificate count cannot charge shared DAG work.

**Changed target:** retire local path pivots unless full Low completeness is used. Investigate the sound-complete certificate-cover DAG, where every Low table must be covered by an accepting cube and every cube remains inside `SIZE(<s2)`. The exact enumeration upper remains `O(N*2^(O(N^beta)))`; ordinary OPS and native fusion frontiers are unchanged. Full proof and literature audit: `research/C424_HIGH_TABLE_PIVOT_FAILURE_AND_COMPLETENESS_BARRIER_2026-09-30.md`.
