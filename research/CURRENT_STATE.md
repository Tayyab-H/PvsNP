## Established ceilings to respect

The following are treated as established results from the prior project record and as hard constraints on new arguments:

- **One anchor:** \(\rho_w=d_U(w)-1\le N-1\). A single anchor cannot supply superlinear growth. The local chain argument gives the upper bound: intersect the \(d_U(w)\) matching literals in sequence; there are \(d_U(w)-1\) pair rules.
- **Fractional cover:** \(\rho^*\le 8N+1\) for the project measure. A purely fractional dual cannot prove a superlinear integral lower bound; a successful cover argument must use a growing integrality gap or another non-fractional invariant.
- **A fixed family:** any fixed family \(\mathcal H\) of \(R\) semi-filters has an integral cover of size \(O(N\log(R+1))\). Given a fractional cover of total weight \(W\le8N+1\), sample pairs from its normalized weights; each filter is hit with probability at least \(1/W\), so \(O(W\log(R+1))\) samples cover all \(R\) filters. Thus any fixed-family lower-bound construction targeting \(N^{1+\varepsilon}\) must involve at least \(\exp(\Omega(N^\varepsilon))\) filters (up to constants in the upper bound).

The earlier local ledger did not contain pinpoint proof references for the fractional-cover theorem; this state records it as an established project result per the supplied research record, and the next audit should attach its original derivation to the claim ledger. The rounding consequence is conditional on a fractional cover of the fixed family at hand. It does **not** give a global upper bound for \(\rho\): \(\rho\) ranges over every semi-filter extension, and a pair that violates the current least closure may only add a nonempty intersection and leave a larger preserving closure. No argument below depends on an unverified strengthening of the stated coefficient.

## Exact closure-game formulation

For a proposed semantic pair list \(Q=((E_i,H_i))_{i=1}^q\), let \(T_i=E_i\cap H_i\). For each anchor \(w\), begin with the upward closure of its matching literal slices. Let \(x_i(w)\) mean that rule \(i\)'s consequence \(T_i\) enters the least closure. Its least fixed point is determined by
\[
x_i(w)=\left(a_i(w)\vee\bigvee_{j:T_j\subseteq E_i}x_j(w)\right)
\wedge
\left(b_i(w)\vee\bigvee_{j:T_j\subseteq H_i}x_j(w)\right),
\]
where \(a_i(w)=1\) iff some matching literal slice is contained in \(E_i\), and \(b_i(w)=1\) iff some matching literal slice is contained in \(H_i\). Each seed predicate is a disjunction of signed truth-table bits; a proper endpoint contains at most one polarity per coordinate. Start at the all-zero state and iterate; at most \(q\) state bits flip, so the least fixed point is reached after at most \(q\) strict rounds. The closure derives \(\emptyset\) iff some active rule has \(T_i=\emptyset\). If it does not, the least closure itself is a proper semi-filter above \(w\) preserved by every pair in \(Q\). Hence the central question is exactly whether this cyclic two-premise system can activate an empty rule on every low-circuit anchor.

For the dense OPS high side, every two-coordinate cylinder contains a high table. Thus an empty-consequence rule cannot activate directly from the initial literal slices; a contradiction proof needs a nonempty bootstrap. Unfolding a round-\(t\) proof gives at most \(2^t\) literal leaves, while C-03 forces \(N-o(N)\) distinct coordinates, so \(t\ge\log_2N-o(\log N)\) (C-72). This is a depth bound, not a superlinear rule-count bound.

The raw surviving-anchor count is not a valid potential: one pair can affect \(N\) or more anchors (C-09). The distance \(\delta_Q(w)\) to empty closure is valid per anchor, but C-73 refutes the simple static-weight strategy: for every distribution on low anchors, some literal-pair rule at \(Q=\varnothing\) saves one rule for at least \(1/4-o(1)\) of its mass. The next invariant must charge shared intermediate intersections once, rather than seek tiny additive marginal progress for every pair. Endpoints remain arbitrary semantic sets, so containment relations \(T_j\subseteq E_i,H_i\) cannot be compressed by an endpoint-description argument. Details and the exact obstruction are in [the closure-game report](FUSION_CLOSURE_GAME_2026-09-26.md).

## Artificial gap calibration

Existing exact models isolate two opposite behaviors:

- Shared-core edge filters reduce to graph cuts: for \(K_m\), the integral cover is \(\lceil\log_2m\rceil\), while the fractional cut cover is about 2 (audit Proposition 18.18). This gives a growing but only logarithmic gap, and it does not satisfy the full anchor condition.
- Co-singleton filters have exact cover \(\binom N2\), but every pair covers at most one filter, so the fractional optimum is also quadratic; they miss the linear fractional ceiling and the anchor condition (audit Proposition 18.19).

These are useful controls, not the required artificial model. No model currently meets all of: per-anchor \(\le N-1\), global fractional \(O(N)\), and global integral \(N^{1+\varepsilon}\), while retaining arbitrary semantic pairs and the anchor literals.

## C-91 - SCC-sensitive unrolling

First delete each rule self-support term: T_i=E_i intersect H_i lies in both endpoints, but these self-loops do not change the least fixed point. In the resulting loop-free support graph, let r_C be SCC sizes and e_C internal side-support incidences. ## C-92/C-93 - Reduce the recursive carrier graph

C-92 is a proved recurrence pruning: after self-loop deletion, strict non-cover carrier supports can be removed from both sides because retained cover edges force every deleted lower-carrier state to imply the upper-carrier state at every fixed point. ## C-94 - Escape supports are the only feedback source

After the C-91/C-92/C-93 reductions, strict subcarrier supports are exactly the shared two-sided Hasse-cover signals. Any remaining dependency outside the inclusion order is one-sided, since support on both endpoints would imply T_j subseteq T_i. Factoring the shared cover signal gives H_t=K_t OR D_t, where D_t contains the candidate-specific escape conjunctions. The carrier Hasse graph is acyclic, so every directed cycle uses an escape edge. Replacing a round of H by upward closure of D preserves the fixed points; the macro iteration stabilizes after at most s+1 rounds, where s is the number of distinct escape-source carriers. This yields a separator circuit O(qN+(s+1)(xi+kappa+q)), with xi escape incidences and kappa carrier-cover edges. No bound on s or xi, and no superlinear cover lower bound, is known. The next target is escape-alternative non-shareability.

## C-98 - Conditional crypto calibration of shared-DAG hardness

A nonuniform-secure one-bit PRF with per-key evaluation size lambda^d would imply SepCirc(Y,Z) > N^C for every constant C, at the project gap s1=N^beta/(cn), s2=N^beta. ## C-99 - The cryptographic benchmark is not an independent separation route

Under the nonuniform-secure PRF assumption of C-98, NP cannot be contained in P/poly. Otherwise MCSP, which is in NP, would have polynomial-size nonuniform circuits. Fixing its threshold at s2 gives a separator for Y versus Z; evaluating that separator after N=poly(lambda) PRF queries breaks security. Keep it as a model calibration, not an independent proof route. Uniform PRF security does not rule out arbitrary nonuniform h_n circuits.

## C-100 - Batched ORs sharpen the escape compiler, but leave the barrier

The \(2q\) seed clauses, \(2q\) escape-support ORs, and \(p\) carrier down-set ORs can each be batched by precomputing all subset ORs on input blocks of \(\Theta(\log r)\) bits. Taking the minimum with the sparse incidence/Hasse implementations gives
\[
S_{\rm rect}=O\!\left(qN/\log N+(s+1)\left[q+\min\{\xi,q(1+s/\log(q+1))\}+\min\{p+\kappa,p+p^2/\log(p+1)\}\right]\right).
\]
Here \(q\ge N-o(N)\), \(p\) is the number of carriers, \(s\) the number of distinct escape sources, \(\xi\) their candidate-side incidences, and \(\kappa\) the carrier Hasse edges. Thus the worst-case compiler improves from \(O(q^3)\) to \(O(q^3/\log q)\); the separator lower-bound scale needed to force \(q>N^{1+\epsilon}\) improves from \(N^{3+3\epsilon+\delta}\) to asymptotically above \(N^{3+3\epsilon}/\log N\). This is only a logarithmic compiler refinement, with no new lower bound or P-vs-NP proof. See C-100 in the bridge report.
