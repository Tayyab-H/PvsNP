## C-490 - complement-lift shells do not escape the locality barrier

The complement lift Lambda(g)(x,z)=g(x) XOR z is an elementary self-embedding; its paired noise shell is not a circuit lower bound or a demonstrated locality-barrier escape. The OPS magnification theorem concerns ordinary total circuits with its exact fixed-epsilon, all-sufficiently-small-fixed-beta quantifiers. Chen and coauthors formal locality theorems apply to named magnification frontiers and restricted models such as oracle-augmented Formula-XOR; this cycle establishes neither that one of those techniques applies here nor immunity from it. ## C-489 - a paired noise shell is a construction, not a barrier escape

For balanced Low centers, almost every weight-preserving neighbor at radius Theta(s2) is High by counting. ## C-488 - paired endpoint distributions do not escape known barriers

The endpoint-pair error lemma is an exact distributional reformulation: a structured mixture on forced Low/High endpoints with positive error against every size-S ordinary circuit rules out a separator. The balanced k-junta/High pair shares Hamming weight but an O(N) repeated-block test separates it, so matching easy observables alone does not create hardness. ## C-487 - precise cryptographic quantifiers; no universal barrier claim

The separator-specific ensemble theorem is a sufficient ordinary-gate lower-bound mechanism, but it is still a distributional hardness premise. A single efficient sampler with constant indistinguishability against every polynomial circuit degree yields weak one-wayness by inverter verification; fixed beta only tests degree q_beta=(1+epsilon)/beta+o(1), so the OWF transfer is unavailable. Razborov-Rudich is conditional and applies to broad natural-property methods; the Chen et al. locality barrier targets specified lower-bound techniques that extend to the oracle circuits produced by magnification. Neither is an impossibility theorem for arbitrary all-extension proofs. The Shannon-Lupanov k-junta example defeats entropy as a hard-ensemble proxy by an O(N) test, not the full distributional criterion. ## C-486 - only the strong sampler claim reaches standard PRGs

For a fixed beta, the well-formed circuit-description sampler has seed length m=Theta(N^beta), output length N=m^(1/beta+o(1)), and poly(m) generation cost. Negligible indistinguishability against every nonuniform poly(m)-size circuit would make it a standard polynomial-stretch PRG and imply one-way functions. This is not a barrier to proving the exact Gap-MCSP lower bound: the C-481 distributional sufficient condition is weaker, and a direct all-extension proof need not produce an efficient sampler. ## C-485 - MCSP-vs-PRF is established and conditional here

C-485 instantiates the standard fact that a correct MCSP distinguisher separates pseudorandom-function truth tables from uniform tables. The parameter fit puts every PRF truth table below the exact OPS s1 threshold while the separator's acceptance set is contained in L_s2. The method is an assumption-based implication, not a new unconditional lower bound and not a natural-proof-barrier escape. ## C-477 - Korten mirror-set initializations fail even after subrectangle restriction

## C-465 - random-table density and average-case MCSP are not a locality barrier

Circuit counting shows a uniform truth table, and almost every completion of any `O(N^beta)`-coordinate cylinder, is formally High with probability `1-2^(-N+O(N^beta*n))`. Every OPS separator therefore induces a zero-error average solver for low-threshold MCSP: output NO when the separator says NO and abstain otherwise. It succeeds on almost every uniform table. This connects the target to established average-case MCSP/natural-property work, but its abstaining average solver may fail on promised High inputs, so the transfer is one-way. It is a project-derived consequence and a possible lower-bound target, not a new barrier or a frontier improvement. The Chen et al. locality barrier remains a separate, method-specific restriction. ## C-454 - local reductions and the magnification locality barrier are distinct

Allender–Ilango–Vafa's Theorem 1 directly excludes nonuniform AC0 many-one reductions from PARITY to `eta(theta)-GapMCSP` for every `eta=o(theta)`. Since the fixed-beta OPS thresholds have `s1/s2=1/(c log N)`, the OPS promise is a special case after setting `theta=floor(N^beta)` and slightly relaxing its low threshold by a constant factor. This gives a project-specific locality corollary for source maps. Chen et al.'s “locality barrier” addresses a different obstruction: known lower-bound techniques for weak models can extend to the bounded-fan-in oracle circuits supplied by magnification reductions. Neither result rules out a general ordinary-circuit proof or an arbitrary-depth costed map. See C-454 and the primary sources linked there.

## C-472 - Parseval-tail recognition is a project result, not a full separator

The stable predicate accepts tables whose Walsh support has span dimension at most m and whose squared coefficient energy outside that span is at most `4Nr`. Parseval proves every accepted table is within `4r` flips of an m-linear-junta and hence has complexity `<s1`; coefficient separation proves it accepts all radius-r flips of that subfamily. Its `O(N log^3 N)` circuit rejects High tables, but misses Low ANDs of `m+1` parities. This is not a locality-barrier argument; it is an incomplete one-sided recognizer. ## C-471 - a spectral cutoff tradeoff is method-specific

Thresholded Walsh-support span rank can be computed in `N polylog N` gates and exactly recognizes functions of m linear forms. It does not yield an OPS separator: cutoff `<2r` rejects a forced-YES r-point perturbation of a bent junta, while cutoff `>=2r` accepts a high table by a Hoeffding-plus-counting argument. ## C-470 - robust geometry is compatible with linear gates

A repetition-code family of `2^{Theta(s1)}` Low truth tables has minimum distance `L=N/k`. For fixed `beta<1/2`, radius `r_in=Theta(s1/n)` balls around its slack anchors are forced Low, while the larger `r_out=Theta(N^beta/n)` neighborhood stays below `s2`; blockwise majority/population counts recognize the latter in `O(N)` ordinary gates. Its support is `2^{Theta(N^beta)}`, and every actual High table lies outside. This does not solve the OPS promise: the test rejects other Low tables. It refutes geometry-only lower-bound charges but does not address Chen et al.'s magnification locality barrier, nor does it give a barrier to arbitrary circuit lower bounds. Details: [C-470](C470_ROBUST_LOW_SUBFAMILY_HAS_LINEAR_SIZE_RECOGNIZER_2026-10-01.md).

## C-466 - natural-property restatement is not the magnification locality barrier

C-466 recasts an OPS separator as a dense property disjoint from size-s1 circuits, and proves a decision-tree accepting-path bound by table interpolation. This is the standard natural-property lens, not a new barrier. Hirahara-Santhanam Proposition 12 treats a related size family 2^k/k^(omega(1)) under fixed rescalings; OPS's 2^(beta*n)/(10n) is not an exact same-parameter instance. The Chen et al. locality barrier concerns known lower-bound techniques extending to oracle-augmented weak circuits; it neither proves nor rules out this direct dense-property lower bound. ## C-453 - AND-product repetition does not amplify to OPS-High

The C-452 OR example survives arbitrary independent AND repetition. On `q=r(n+1)` inputs, the repeated simple extension has exact size `q-1`; the repeated valid-key NO function is nonmonotone and essential, so its size is at least `q`, but an explicit construction uses at most `3q` gates. Both therefore remain below `2^(beta q)/(c q)` eventually. This is a precise obstruction to the direct-product amplifier, not to nonlinear/output-hidden maps. It is neither a locality barrier nor a circuit lower bound. See [C-453](C453_DIRECT_PRODUCT_DOES_NOT_AMPLIFY_SIMPLE_EXTENSION_GAP_2026-10-01.md).

## C-452 - exact simple-extension hardness does not imply an OPS gap

For `f=OR_n`, the simple YES `OR_{n+1}` has circuit size `n`, while `OR_n(x) XOR (x_1 AND y)` is a nondegenerate NO with a valid key and size between `n+1` and `n+5`. On its `2^(n+1)`-bit truth table, even this NO instance is below `N^beta/(c log N)` for every fixed beta eventually. This is a concrete failure of identity encoding after key/essentiality preprocessing, not an impossibility result for transformations that amplify the gap. Carmosino-Dang-Jackman establish polynomial-time total OR- and XOR-Simple-Extension algorithms under their optimal-circuit structural conditions; their possible multiplexer direction has no demonstrated ordinary OPS transfer. See [C-452](C452_SIMPLE_EXTENSION_HAS_NO_OPS_GAP_2026-10-01.md) and [primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16903).

## C-451 - dual-rail monotonicity preserves sharing but not source monotonicity

Any ordinary separator lifts to a monotone circuit on `2N` signed rails with at most twice as many internal gates, exactly preserving DAG fan-out. But the legal signed truth-table encodings form an antichain, so every labeling of them admits a monotone extension; the full-promise monotone DNF is an explicit extension at cost `O(N|L_s1|)`. Existing monotone lower bounds, including spread-matching bounds, therefore need a separate endpoint-preserving map whose negative rails preserve source monotonicity; ordinary table composition does not guarantee this. This is a model equivalence and a route-specific transfer obstruction, not a new locality barrier or blanket impossibility theorem. See [C-451](C451_SIGNED_RAIL_MONOTONE_LIFT_AUDIT_2026-10-01.md), [Rao TR26-129](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/129/), and [Chen et al. TR19-168](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/168/).

## C-450 - codebook pullback is an endpoint issue, not a new barrier

The exact promise is the codebook sandwich `L_s1 ⊆ F^{-1}(1) ⊆ L_s2`. Pulling `F` back along descriptions of circuits of size at most `s2` supplies no forced NO values; descriptions longer than `s2` can still compute Low tables. A direct fan-in-two table evaluator costs at most `O(Nt(n+t))` gates for size-`t` descriptions, but a source-specific hard-output construction and full generator budget remain necessary. This route diagnostic is not an impossibility theorem and neither bypasses nor strengthens the Chen et al. locality barrier. Direct and source routes still lack the required common-fixed-ε ordinary lower bound. See [C-450](C450_FIRST_PRINCIPLES_CODEBOOK_SANDWICH_AUDIT_2026-10-01.md).

## C-449 - implicit hardness materialization budget

Conditional NP-hardness of implicit Gap-MCSP is a result about sampler descriptions. Directly expanding a total full-support sampler with `k`-bit addresses and `q` random bits costs at most `O(2^{k+q} poly(size(E),k,q))`; this does not prove a lower bound against more efficient encoders. The published no-case circuit guarantee is subexponential relative to `k`, while explicit Gap-MCSP needs complexity above `2^{βk}`. Source composition further pays the generator. This blocks the direct transfer only; it is not a locality or universal impossibility theorem. See [C-449](C449_IMPLICIT_MCSP_MATERIALIZATION_TRANSFER_AUDIT_2026-10-01.md).

## C-448 - affine orbit symmetrization pays for the whole group

Address-affine precomposition changes circuit complexity by at most `O(n²)`, so ANDing all transformed copies of a separator gives an invariant extension on shrunken Low/High cores. The direct circuit costs `|AGL(n,2)|S+O(|AGL|)=Θ(N^{n+1}S)`, too large for the OPS near-linear regime. Parity and C-160's Hamming-threshold calibration show invariance alone is not a superlinear gate charge. This retires direct orbit averaging only; it does not rule out a structure-aware symmetrizer. ## C-446 - pairwise disagreement covers erase the computation

Every valid separator induces a gate-path protocol: start at the differing output values on a Low/High pair, descend to a differing child of each AND/OR/NOT gate, and end at a table coordinate where the inputs differ. Shared circuit subcomputations become shared protocol states. This is a correct circuit-to-protocol translation, not a lower bound. If a proposed invariant remembers only which endpoint pairs disagree at each state, the whole relation is covered by the `N` coordinate-disagreement sets and an `O(N)` OR tree. The invariant must retain the one-sided Boolean functions and their gate composition; otherwise its state complexity cannot exceed the linear relaxation. See [C-446](C446_FIRST_PRINCIPLES_AUDIT_AND_GATE_PATH_MODEL_2026-10-01.md).

## C-445 - constructive gate-elimination refuters do not bridge to universal High

Recent refuters produce a mismatch for a supplied circuit at linear-in-input-length size bounds. GapMCSP's High endpoint excludes all circuits up to `s2=2^(βn)` when table length is `N=2^n`. A per-circuit refuter does not provide a short proof that it works for every description, and the audited results do not establish an exponential-in-`n` circuit lower bound. MUX lower bounds remain linear in the number of data inputs. This closes the direct refuter transfer, not gate elimination or the OPS target generally. See [C-445](C445_CONSTRUCTIVE_REFUTERS_MISS_OPS_SCALE_2026-10-01.md).

## C-444 - feasible interpolation does not reverse the separator-to-proof direction

The direct NP certificate listing a mismatch for every size-`s2` circuit has superpolynomial length: a structurally chained family gives at least `s2!` descriptions. A succinct selector still requires universal correctness. A sound proof system does yield an NP subfamily `W⊆NO` of tables with short QBF refutations; a lower bound for separating `W` from YES would transfer to all full-promise separators. No such family or lower bound is known. Standard feasible interpolation extracts a circuit from a short proof, while this project needs either a direct pair-separator lower bound or a reverse small-separator-to-proof theorem. This is a route-specific obstacle, not a barrier for all proof methods. See [C-444](C444_FEASIBLE_INTERPOLATION_AUDIT_AND_CERTIFIED_HIGH_SUBFAMILY_2026-10-01.md).

## C-442 - the missing bridge is a superlinear charge on shared recognition work

The C-442 retrospective reconciles the project's main proof families: support and sensitivity constrain input dependence; certificates and anti-checkers constrain semantic witnesses; restricted models expose lower bounds but lose arbitrary sharing in transfer; hard-source routes need exact promise preservation and a gate budget. None currently gives a superlinear lower bound for every total ordinary DAG separator. A proved budget lemma sharpens the source-transfer issue: if a generated table itself decodes its hard source output in `B` gates, then the generator already costs at least source hardness minus `B`; source composition cannot force a separator larger than `B` by itself. For the C-441 prefix table, `B=O(N)`, so retire that map as a superlinear source route. This is not a universal barrier or a Gap-MCSP bound. OPS remains stronger than `P!=NP`; no known locality, natural-proofs, relativization, or ZFC result proves every possible route impossible. Full synthesis: [C-442](C442_FIRST_PRINCIPLES_REASSESSMENT_2026-10-01.md).

## C-441 - prefix table already reveals the source output

Ren--Williams' Lemma 3.2 places the hard source output in a fixed segment of the lexicographically largest satisfying assignment. The all-prefix extension table reveals that largest assignment: a binary trie decoder chooses a feasible right child whenever possible, otherwise the left child. This costs `O(N)` total fan-in-two gates. Therefore any generator of the entire prefix table costs at least the source lower bound minus `O(N)`, and a source-hardness contradiction through this map can only apply to separators below linear size. The promise gap for the actual table is not proved. This closes one map-specific budget route; it is not a no-go theorem for OPS. Full proof: [C-441](C441_PREFIX_TABLE_ALREADY_CONTAINS_SOURCE_OUTPUT_2026-10-01.md).

## C-440 - prefix existence does not transfer canonical-witness hardness

For a formula with a unique length-r satisfying assignment w, the truth table over prefixes p that marks `p` iff it extends to a satisfying assignment has only r+1 positive coordinates. Equality-to-hardwired-prefix tests give an `O(r^2)` total-gate circuit, which is OPS-YES at length `N=2^(r+1)` for every fixed beta eventually. This defeats the generic prefix-tree batching idea even if the selected witness is hard as a function of the source input. The one-query computational-history gadget further shows an `O(L^2)` prefix-extension table for both a satisfiable query `phi(z)=z_1` with many witnesses and an unsatisfiable query. It does not analyze the full Ren--Williams `C_x` family or preclude another source map. The local PCP verifier remains Low on both sides; generator cost is separate. No frontier change. Full proof: [C-440](C440_PREFIX_TREE_TABLE_IS_LOW_FOR_UNIQUE_WITNESSES_2026-10-01.md).

## C-439 - direct sum is not a generic shared-gate charge

The query-by-query circuit composition in C-438 is a valid serial-substitution upper bound, not a lower bound for joint computation. A published GF(2) matrix example shows that Boolean circuits can compute n copies of a fixed linear map jointly by matrix multiplication at `O(n^2.38)` gates, even though a counting-chosen individual map needs `Omega(n^2/log n)` gates. Thus a source-query transfer cannot multiply query count by an individual lower bound without a source-specific theorem. C-439 redirects toward a single-table promise-preserving map for the entire hard source computation or a proved joint compiler. The OPS threshold lower bound remains open; the locality barrier is still technique-specific. Full audit and citations: [C-439](C439_FIRST_PRINCIPLES_AUDIT_AND_JOINT_COMPOSITION_2026-10-01.md).

## C-437 - unrestricted source hardness helps only after the table map is paid for

Ren–Williams' near-maximum lower bound for `E^{prMA}/1` is against ordinary circuits, so it avoids the monotone-model mismatch. A direct composition theorem gives source circuit size `R+B+S`; a polynomial-output, polynomial-gate reduction to the exact OPS promise would make `S=N^(1+epsilon)` far below the source bound `Omega(2^m/m)` and would settle the target. The attempted local-history map fails: at subexponential table length, a polynomial-size address predicate makes every table Low, and at polynomial table length no High-side circuit lower bound follows. Any prMA queries also need an ordinary-circuit simulation. This is not an impossibility theorem for reductions; it specifies the missing transfer. The near-maximum source result itself is not a GapMCSP result. No quantitative frontier change. Full audit: [C-437](C437_EPRMA_NEARMAX_SOURCE_TRANSFER_WINDOW_2026-10-01.md).

## C-436 - monotone hardness does not survive arbitrary separator composition

The spread-matching result gives `exp(Ω(sqrt(n)))` monotone-circuit hardness and was already used in the C-261/C-266 native cyclic source route; C-436 tests only a transfer to ordinary OPS separators. Such a separator may use negations, so composition gives only an ordinary circuit, and matching itself has polynomial-size ordinary circuits. Dual-rail encoding does not restore monotonicity in the original edge variables. The candidate-verifier table on `D=Theta(n log n)` bits has poly(n)-size circuits for both graph outcomes, below the OPS low cutoff `2^(beta D)/(cD)`, so it fails to produce High tables. This is a model-transfer obstruction for the tested construction, not a theorem ruling out all reductions. C-436's broader audit confirms the recurring magnification issue: known restricted lower bounds help only when the exact reduction and composition preserve their model, while the locality barrier warns against methods that extend to small fan-in oracle gates. No quantitative frontier change. Full audit: [C-436](C436_FIRST_PRINCIPLES_AUDIT_AND_MONOTONE_TRANSFER_2026-10-01.md).

## C-435 - a standard communication measure controls sharing only linearly

Cut GF(2) rank yields a rigorous total-gate inequality for arbitrary DAGs: a party holding one side of an input partition sends at most two input-bit occurrences per fan-in-two gate to the other party, which evaluates all shared gates; deterministic communication bounds matrix rank by `2^(2S+1)`. But log-rank is at most `N/2`, and `AND_i(x_i OR y_i)` realizes full rank with `N-1` gates. Parity, equality, sparse checks, and global block-parity relations are also O(N). This is a standard tool applied to the sharing question, not a new superlinear theorem. It does not bypass the locality barrier. The exact enumeration separator is still `O(N*2^(O(N^beta)))`; ordinary and native frontiers are unchanged. Full proof: [C-435](C435_COMMUNICATION_RANK_CHARGES_SHARING_BUT_CANNOT_EXCEED_LINEAR_2026-10-01.md).

2025–2026 literature check: Atserias–Müller give distinguisher-based general magnification and a uniform MCSP consequence; this does not supply the OPS nonuniform ordinary-DAG lower bound. Goldberg–Juvekar–Kabanets TR26-091 gives conditional hardness for sampler-input ImpMCSP under crypto and proof-system assumptions, a different problem and model. Neither changes the present frontier.

## C-434 - promise transfer succeeds for comparator circuits, then stops at sharing

The published Cavalar–Lu local-PRG argument for exact MCSP needs only that all generator outputs are Low and that at least half of a random restricted block is High. It therefore proves separate gate and active-wire lower bounds `N^(1+0.455 beta)` for the exact OPS gap promise at each fixed `0<beta<1/2`, even though the middle band is unconstrained. For gates, the proof counts up to two active wires per gate before averaging. The gain tends to zero with beta, so it does not meet OPS's single fixed-`epsilon` quantifier even if transferred. This does not escape the locality barrier or address ordinary circuits: comparator gates have bounded fanout, and unfolding an arbitrary DAG pays `2^mu`, where `mu` is its gate-to-gate cycle rank. The resulting `S*2^mu>=N^(1+0.455 beta)/O(1)` is only a sharing tradeoff. No ordinary separator lower bound follows. Full derivation: [C-434](C434_GAPMCSP_COMPARATOR_BOUND_DOES_NOT_LIFT_TO_SHARED_DAGS_2026-09-30.md).

## C-433 - lambda can shrink after edge indexing, but the OPS ratio still fails

C-433 rechecks the cost terms after C-432. The edge-index decoder removes the `poly(n^tau)` tuple-membership cost in HI Lemma V.2; the NO-side correction is `poly(n*tau)`, and the explicit source edge list has `M<=n^(O(log tau))`. Retuning `lambda=n^(A log tau)` with fixed large A dominates both, improves `log s` to `Theta(log tau log n)`, retains the certified gap `Omega(tau)`, and keeps the reduction quasipolynomial. The exact source range is `gamma<tau<=(log n)^(1/gamma)` for universal `gamma>=1`; hence `tau/log s=o(1)` and the required OPS ratio `g/log s>c/beta` still fails. This is a parameterized conditional reduction improvement, not an ordinary circuit lower bound or an upper bound on true hardness. Do not repeat edge-code tuning; a new source/gap or direct separator argument is needed. Full report: [C-433](C433_REPARAMETERIZED_HI_GAP_STILL_MISSES_OPS_2026-09-30.md).

## C-432 - explicit edge-list reindexing removes one barrier but not the OPS threshold fit

The edge tuple in HI can be replaced by an index into the explicitly listed `E`: decode `e_j`, include the lookup gates in completeness, and use the existing pointwise-per-edge hybrid for soundness after hardcoding `j`. ## Representation independence and nonuniformity

The active theorem targets arbitrary Boolean circuit separators, not one solver architecture. A polynomial-time SAT decider induces polynomial-size circuits, and the fusion theorem connects those circuits to the project cover measure. Nonuniform advice is included.

For the exact family conversion, restrict the separator to Gamma=Y union Z, express input bits and negations as the 2N generators, and translate AND gates to intersections. Constants can be formed from complementary literals with O(1) overhead. The argument does not assume a witness, search tree, or preferred encoding.

## Natural proofs

The magnification implication is published, but this does not show that a proposed proof of its premise avoids every natural-proofs obstacle. Test largeness, constructivity, and PRG usefulness against the actual property on N-bit truth tables. Do not dismiss or endorse a proof solely by labeling it natural.

## Relativization and algebrization

The near-linear leaf bound is combinatorial and relativizing in spirit; it does not establish the superlinear theorem. Any candidate that treats an oracle as a literal generator or changes only the input encoding should be tested against oracle worlds. No oracle-resistant ingredient for O-1 is currently identified.

## Proof complexity

The semi-filter closure is an explicit refutation process, but the target is a circuit lower bound, not a lower bound in a fixed proof system. A proof-search or proof-length argument needs a size-preserving bridge to arbitrary circuits; none is currently available.

## Advice, preprocessing, sharing, and recomputation

The measure includes nonuniform circuits and arbitrary DAG sharing through the intersection-complexity theorem. Recomputing rather than storing does not evade a circuit-size lower bound. A proof that counts only proof-DAG leaves, one decomposition, or a selected filter family does not account for alternative circuit computations.

## Uniformity and promise parameters

Gap-MCSP is a promise problem. Use Gamma=Y union Z or prove the chosen totalization is legitimate. Track n=log N, fixed beta, s1=2^(beta n)/(c n), and s2=2^(beta n) separately. Magnification quantifies over every sufficiently small fixed beta; one parameter sequence is insufficient.

## Anti-checker existence versus synthesis

Finite minimax and sampling already give short anti-checkers for each hard truth table. This is compatible with the fixed-sample counterexample because the equilibrium distribution depends on the table. A proof that merely improves the existential support size does not address the OPS barrier. The unresolved object is the map from the full truth table to a valid support, with a quantitative circuit-size lower bound.

For a proposed support, anti-checking is coNP-verifiable; extending a partial list is in $\Sigma_2^P$. Under $P=NP$ this becomes polynomial-time searchable, but the polynomial degree has no reason to be near one in the truth-table length. OPS's conditional near-linear selector uses stronger nonuniform approximate counting. This is a parameter barrier, not a quantifier contradiction.

## Sparse hard-support caveat

C-23 gives a near-linear selector on almost every uniformly random weight-$s_2$ table. Thus the sparse-support counting argument C-18 cannot by itself imply a lower bound on selector circuits. The remaining sparse worst cases are supports inside sparse low-circuit regions; outputting all positives and a fixed hitting set does not handle their low-density supersets. Any claimed lower bound must address those cases rather than a typical random support.

## Separate 2025 uniform magnification result

Atserias and Müller prove a uniform MCSP approximation magnification theorem whose lower-bound premise implies $P\ne NP^{\oplus P}$. This is a real alternative target and avoids the specific nonuniform selector, but that class separation is not currently a proof of $P\ne NP$. Keep the route separate unless a valid implication between the classes is established.


## Near-total input dependence does not charge routing

C-34/C-38 are representation independent: any valid selector depends on $N-o(N)$ inputs and, by the circuit-graph connectivity count, needs at least $N-o(N)$ fan-in-two gates. This is still only linear because a bounded-fan-in circuit can aggregate information from all inputs in linear size. A candidate invariant must measure how addresses and labels are routed so every small circuit is hit. Do not present sensitivity or dependency count as a superlinear obstruction.

C-35/C-36 sharpen the distributional and representation audit. A fixed sample is valid on almost every uniformly random table, so average-case success does not control worst-case exceptions. Every robust affine sketch of dimension $k=o(N)$ also fails as an address mechanism, even with an unrestricted decoder. This is not a lower bound against arbitrary circuits: nonlinear address feedback can correlate selected coordinates with sketch fibers. Any claimed extension must bound this feedback without assuming a selector architecture.

C-38 raises the universal fan-in-two selector lower bound to $N-o(N)$ gates, but C-39's priority encoder meets the linear scale for the two constant hypotheses. Thus the connectivity bound is essentially sharp for simple classes. A superlinear proof must exploit simultaneous avoidance of the full exponentially large low-circuit class, not merely input dependence or positive/negative witness selection.

C-51 converts the majority-error lemma into a dense graph of pairs of low-circuit functions that err together on many inputs. This is representation independent, but the induced max-score contraction is weaker than the dual-margin result, and an abstract common-core class has the same overlap property with a constant-size selector. The June 2026 Goldberg–Juvekar–Kabanets result proves conditional NP-hardness for gap ImpMCSP and full-support learning under subexponential iO and proof-system assumptions. ## Dual-witness route audit (C-40)

The minimax margin and random-support argument apply to the full circuit class and do not assume a solver representation. They are, however, existence statements. This does not cross a barrier or imply a lower bound. ## Quantifier collapse is not quantitative circuit collapse (C-44)

Under P=NP, the polynomial hierarchy collapses to P, so the $\Sigma_2^P$ relation for finding an anti-checker list becomes polynomial-time searchable on promised high tables. This yields some polynomial circuit family, but its degree is unrestricted. The OPS magnification theorem requires a specific near-linear $N^{1+\epsilon}$ bound; class collapse by itself gives no exponent control. This separates a decision-class implication from the resource-preserving bridge actually needed.

## Approximate counting reconstructs the conditional selector, not a lower bound (C-48)

The version-space potential can be optimized by relative approximate counts, not exact #P values. Stockmeyer places the approximation in BPP$^{NP}$; under $NP\subseteq P/poly$, the short-transcript approximation has deterministic polynomial-size circuits after amplification and nonuniform fixing of random bits. Parallel evaluation over all $N$ candidate points and $O(s\log(s+n))$ rounds gives the familiar $N^{1+O(\beta)}$ selector. This is consistent with the OPS magnification argument and confirms that the score is an implementation choice. No lower bound for that chosen implementation constrains selectors using a different computation. Any proof must lower-bound the universal puncturing-certificate map directly or prove a representation-independent reduction to score computation.


## C-52 - Global marginals solve only the first query

A constant-error coordinate exists against the entire low-circuit description set and can be found for every high table by hardwiring an O(n)-sample marginal profile; the selector uses O(Nn) gates. Thus the first greedy point does not inherently require #P counting.

The profile is not a conditional sample. For a residual cell of mass r, conditional frequencies divide joint frequencies by r; a global additive approximation only gives constant relative accuracy while r is above its error scale. An empty empirical version space does not certify that the true one is empty. This is a limitation of this sampling architecture only; arbitrary selectors may avoid these scores.


## C-53 - Relative sampling sharpens but does not close the tail

A multiplicative Chernoff argument over all transcript ranges needs only $O((kn+\log(1/\eta))/(\gamma\rho))$ samples to preserve constant-fraction elimination while residual mass is at least $\rho$. This is better than the inverse-square bound from additive approximation and lets a polynomial-size sample track inverse-polynomial cells.

A surviving description may have mass $1/|\mathcal H|$. At that scale the sample requirement depends on the full description space, which is superpolynomial in $N$ here. The result rules out only the obvious global-sample completion. It neither proves any particular rare cell is reached nor constrains selectors that do not follow this construction.


## C-54 - The sampled prefix stops before interpolation runs out

For any $q=O(n)$ transcript, a DNF on the queried positive points matches all labels using $O(qn)=O(n^2)$ gates, which is below $s_1$ eventually. Therefore no such transcript is an anti-checker. Relative sampling with inverse-polynomial threshold certifies only $O(n)$ constant-contraction rounds, so its guarantee ends while a small consistent circuit is still known to exist. The remaining gap to $\Omega(s_1/n)$ queries is specific to this greedy sample method; it does not imply a circuit lower bound for arbitrary selectors.


## C-55 through C-57 - Witness totality, routing, and output length

Minimax proves a short external teaching set pointwise, but its coNP validity relation does not have a generic small circuit Skolemization. The existential greedy strategy uses exact residual counts; this is an implementation barrier, not evidence that every selector must count. The trace-cylinder lemma shows why output counting is weak: a valid trace is shared by $2^{N-q}$ completions, almost all high at OPS parameters. The external teaching-set length is only pinned between $\Omega(s_1/n)$ and $O(s_1\log(s_1+n))$; shrinking this interval does not by itself bound the circuit that routes $f$ to a valid trace. No standard learning-theory hardness transfers without a reduction for this outside-class, nonminimal, succinct-circuit setting.

## Search-reduction and decoder audit (C-58--C-60)

A reduction to anti-checker selection must handle every valid output, not only the output of a chosen selector. Local changes outside one valid base list leave that list valid (C-58). A proposed repair using one easy-recognizable block per source bit also fails if each omitted-bit approximation is low-circuit and correct off its own disjoint block: two approximants plus a mux compute the high table in $2s_1+\mathrm{poly}(n)$ size (C-59).

This does not establish that every short list forced through a region yields a low-circuit disagreement set contained in that region. The bounded-length hypergraph counterexample in C-59 blocks that inference. Any successful transfer must give a different all-output mechanism and separately prove the high-table promise and resource preservation.

Kannan's theorem leaves both a quantifier gap (the hard language can depend on fixed exponent $k$) and a resource gap. If table generation and output decoding have circuit exponents $b_k,d_k$, table blowup $N=m^{a_k}$ gives composed exponent $e_k=\max\{a_k(1+\epsilon),b_k,d_k\}$. A contradiction requires $k>e_k$, while also ensuring high tables and all-output decoding (C-60). No such reduction is established.

C-61 supplies an additional semantic limit on region-based reductions: with the same dual witness for one high table, each region mandatory for all lists up to $t$ must absorb nearly $3/10$ of its mass. ## C-409 - Full-table PRF security is a conditional natural-property obstruction

The C-409 proof is a direct distributional argument: if a separator accepts all truth tables generated by a low-complexity PRF but rejects almost every uniform table, it distinguishes the PRF from random. Cheraghchi et al.'s local-PRG framework already uses exactly this low-output/pseudorandomness template to obtain MCSP lower bounds in restricted circuit models; C-409 specializes it to the general-circuit OPS gap under an assumed security level. Natural-proofs reasoning explains why such an efficient property conflicts with sufficiently secure PRFs; it does not construct that security. A key of `k=n^2` only aligns the size scales (`N^(1+epsilon)=2^((1+epsilon)sqrt{k})`), and an alpha>1/2 subexponential security hypothesis is stronger than standard polynomial security. No new unconditional method or lower bound results. Sources: Cheraghchi et al., ICALP 2019 local PRG paper; OPS Theorem 1.4; Chen et al. on locality. Full audit: `research/C409_FULL_TABLE_PSEUDORANDOMNESS_CONDITIONAL_GAPMCSP_2026-09-30.md`.

## C-410 - Locality and query-readout boundary

C-410 is a general shared-circuit compiler, not a lower bound: sorting N table records together with q adaptive-query outputs and propagating values through the sorted stream costs O((N+q)log^3(N+q)) gates. It removes the proof's separate O(qN) formatter charge at OPS parameters. This is consistent with the locality barrier: work charged to separate local query projections may be shared globally. Separately, the exact static Anti-Checker Hypothesis from OPS is refuted by Chen et al., Theorem 59/Corollary 61, through an oracle-formula locality lower bound. That refutation is route-specific and does not establish an unrestricted ordinary-circuit lower bound or refute the adaptive OPS anti-checker construction. No natural-proofs or locality barrier is bypassed by C-410; no P-vs-NP frontier changes.

## C-411 - Parameterized locality limit for static anti-checker menus

If a fixed anti-checker menu uses L=N^(2-delta) sets of size q=N^(kappa beta) at OPS high threshold N^beta, the induced AND of Succinct-MCSP oracles has SIZE3 at most N^(2-delta+3*kappa beta+o(1)). ## C-413 - Oracle lookup sharing and exact magnification mismatch

A sort/scan/restore compiler serves Q nonadaptive queries to a fixed K-entry Boolean table in O((Q+K)polylog(Q+K)) ordinary total gates, and d oracle layers add a factor d. This explicitly captures sharing; it is an upper compiler, not a universal lower bound. Random lookup tables require Omega(K/log K) gates by ordinary circuit counting, but C-413 does not show a Gap-MCSP separator computes such a lookup. Ilango's random-oracle reduction is relativized at the target circuit measure and has a constant-factor gap near M/log M. OPS needs the ratio s2/s1=10 log M. Therefore this transfer misses both standard-model and quantitative requirements. Neither OPS's ordinary total-gate lower bound nor the locality barrier is resolved; Chen et al.'s theorem still applies only to its specified oracle/locality methods. Details: research/C413_BATCHED_LOOKUP_CHARGE_FAILS_TO_TRANSFER_TO_OPS_2026-09-30.md.

## C-414 - Output support limits lookup reductions before the separator

If one or t promised N-bit tables are generated from a K-bit indexed source and a postprocessor that may inspect the generated tables and separator outputs, but no raw source bits, returns A_i, then K<=tN+2R where R counts all fan-in-two encoder gates. The composite MUX requires R+tS+B>=K-1. With sublinear-in-K encoder and postprocessor costs, source width per separator call is at most N(1+o(1)); if source width per call is superlinear, the encoder is already Omega(K). Thus the straightforward query-entropy reduction cannot charge a superlinear cost to the separator alone. This is a source-input support theorem, not a barrier against other encodings. OPS's theorem and Chen et al.'s locality theorem are unchanged; the latter still does not constrain arbitrary separators. Full proof: research/C414_PROMISE_EMBEDDING_SUPPORT_CAPACITY_NO_GO_2026-09-30.md.

## C-415 - Exact-extension reductions need exponential no-case hardness

For `M=2^d` table inputs, OPS needs no-case output complexity at least `2^(beta*d)`. The 2026 f-Simple-Extension framework tests exact size `CC(f)+m` and a restriction key; its negative label alone yields no exponential circuit lower bound. The explicit `OR_r(x) OR PARITY_m(y)` construction for `m>=r+2` is a nonmember with a key and all inputs essential, but its circuit size is `O(d)`, so it is a promise-YES table. More generally, adjoining U+6 parity variables to any explicit U-gate nondegenerate base gives a negative extension of O(d) size. Naive OR-products/MUX composition preserve polynomial-in-arity circuit upper bounds. This blocks only the exact-label transfer and naive product amplification; MUX's simple-extension complexity and other total reductions remain open. No OPS lower-bound or locality-barrier consequence follows. Full proof: `research/C415_SIMPLE_EXTENSION_GAP_AMPLIFICATION_FAILS_2026-09-30.md`.
## C-416 - Completion entropy is not a locality or gate lower bound

Circuit counting gives |Low_s1|<=2^(O(M^beta)) at OPS parameters. A partial table with u stars, completed uniformly, hits Low with probability at most 2^(O(M^beta)-u); a general sampler with min-entropy h has bound |Low|2^-h. This is an elementary sampler limitation. It fails to control source-aware correlations: parity, repeated-block tables, sparse parity-check families, and tables assembled from a small seed and a simple suffix relation can all be generated as Low outputs from low-entropy samplers. The argument does not derive a lower bound on any separator gate, does not construct a localized oracle computation, and neither bypasses nor strengthens Chen et al.'s technique-specific locality barrier. The partial f-SEP* predicate asks for some exact simple extension and is not itself an OPS low-completion promise. No ordinary, native, or quantitative P-vs-NP frontier changes. Full analysis: research/C416_PARTIAL_TABLE_COMPLETION_ENTROPY_LIMIT_2026-09-30.md.

## C-417 - Output routing is a model interface, not a locality-barrier bypass

The bitwise/multi-output tradeoff is ordinary circuit accounting. A single-output table generator of R gates gives each fixed table circuit size at most R, but the direct separator composition costs S+MR. A multi-output generator composes at S+R, but the standard gate-only multi-output definition allows designated outputs to be input wires or gate outputs; its M output choices may carry a long router description. Ilango, Loff, and Oliveira explicitly study gate-size circuit minimization for multi-output total functions, including the output-component convention. C-417's two-gate low/high routed example is not an OPS reduction and not a new theorem about that problem. It neither uses localized oracle gates nor bypasses/strengthens Chen et al.'s technique-specific locality barrier. The ordinary OPS exponent and native rho frontiers are unchanged. Sources and exact costs: research/C417_PAIRED_FAMILY_OUTPUT_MODEL_TRADEOFF_2026-09-30.md.

## C-418 - Stronger block-trace avoidance does not imply a hardness transfer

C-418 union-bounds against circuits below OPS `s2`, not only below `s1`. For every fixed `beta<1` and `gamma<1-beta`, it obtains `q=Theta(M^gamma)` blocks such that every nonconstant repeated-block table is High. The induced promise remains easy: equality of the `q` representative bits is decided in `O(q)` gates, and a source map through `R` shared block values has label complexity at most `R+O(q)`. This is a stronger restriction lemma and an explicit counterexample to treating repetition or high output complexity as work forced on a full separator. It is not a general lower-bound method, does not challenge the known locality barrier for specified approximation/local-oracle methods, and makes no native-fusion claim. The full-promise target and all quantitative frontiers remain unchanged. Report: research/C418_HIGH_TRACE_RANDOM_BLOCKS_STILL_EASY_2026-09-30.md.


## C-419 - Balanced-block traces are easy at every fixed dimension

Combining C-418 for `gamma<=beta` with C-408 for `gamma>beta` proves that, for every fixed OPS-small `beta<1/2` and every fixed block exponent `gamma in (0,1)`, some balanced partition has an exact induced-promise selector of `O(M^gamma log^2 M)` gates. The proof handles the actual `s1=M^beta/(10d), s2=M^beta` thresholds and arbitrary sharing; the resulting selector is small because the block pattern promise is simple. This closes the balanced-block restriction family as a hard-trace route, not as a full-promise lower bound. Parity is outside the slice. ## C-420 - Codeword embedding failure and the exact OPS target

C-420's random codeword-hard subspace is compatible with the OPS thresholds but its induced promise is trivial zero testing: `W\{0}` is all High and `0^N` is Low, so `NOR_N` separates it. For a hard kernel-membership source, this forces the shared table generator—not the separator—to pay the source lower bound up to `O(N)`. This is a reduction accounting no-go, not an implication of the locality barrier. ## C-421 - A real separator supplies a certificate, not a compact selector

The reverse proof-DAG extraction is an unconditional ordinary-circuit statement: every High input has a mask of at most `min(N,2S)` coordinates that fixes the separator's output to 0; therefore no Low circuit agrees on that set. Its gate cost is O(S) with shared gates marked once. This avoids assuming a description output or address-wise algorithm, but the mask may have N positions and does not meet OPS's `N^(10 beta)` list length for small beta. Shrinking it while preserving C=0 on every completion is Sigma2^P search because fixed-mask validity is coNP. The OPS anti-checker list only excludes Low circuits; C's arbitrary acceptance on middle tables can force a larger zero-certificate. Parity shows generic certificate width N is possible with O(N) gates. The result neither bypasses nor strengthens the locality barrier and changes no quantitative frontier. See C-421.

## C-422 - Anti-checker existence versus computation

The OPS parameterized game has a constant fractional disagreement value for every High table: if a distribution over Low circuits predicted all N addresses with error below 1/4, a majority of O(n) sampled circuits would compute the High table using fewer than s2 fan-in-two gates. Minimax and an O(log |Low|)=O(N^beta) sample yield a short anti-checker. This is the established Lipton-Young theorem in OPS gate parameters; it is per-input existence, not an efficiently computed selector and not a lower bound on a Gap-MCSP separator.

C-421's separator certificate is stronger semantically (it forces C=0 on every completion) but may have N coordinates. C-422's short anti-checker only excludes Low completions; middle-band completions may still be accepted by C. No bridge from an arbitrary separator's shared evaluation DAG to the fractional strategy was proved. OPS Lemma 4.1 constructs a larger N^(10 beta) list conditionally under NP subseteq P/poly; the locality barrier in Chen et al. remains technique-specific and is not contradicted or bypassed. Ordinary total-gate and native fusion frontiers are unchanged. ## C-429 - transcript-fiber audit

The complete gate transcript of an acyclic fan-in-two circuit induces 2-CNF fibers on its primary inputs. Accepting fibers are majority-closed subsets of SIZE(s2), and a free-variable count yields only a linear bound. An O(N)-gate coordinate-cylinder circuit supplies an actual accepting transcript fiber containing 2^r sparse Low tables.
