# C-235 — Cube-interval calculus for native proof reuse

Date: 27 September 2026  
Scope: make the soundness condition for reused native proofs explicit as an interval law on the Boolean cube. This yields exact endpoint constraints for every compatible splice and for simultaneous substitutions. It is a structural lemma, not a superlinear lower bound.

## 1. Supports are Boolean intervals

Let a proof support be S subset [N] x {0,1}. If S contains both rails at any coordinate, set Q(S)=empty. Otherwise define

P(S)={k:(k,1) in S}, Z(S)={k:(k,0) in S},

and the lower and upper endpoints

ell(S)=1_{P(S)}, u(S)=1_{[N] minus Z(S)}.

Let `Lambda(x)={(k,x_k):k in [N]}` be the signed encoding of a table. With the coordinatewise order on Boolean tables,

Q(S)={x:S subset Lambda(x)}={x:ell(S) <= x <= u(S)}.

Thus a consistent support is exactly a Boolean subcube interval, and its free coordinates are those where the two endpoints differ. For arbitrary supports S,T, including inconsistent ones,

    Q(S union T)=Q(S) intersect Q(T).                         (1)

The endpoint operations are

    ell(S union T)=ell(S) OR ell(T),
    u(S union T)=u(S) AND u(T),                               (2)

when the union is consistent; if it is inconsistent, the intersection in (1) is empty.

## 2. Exact soundness law for a proof and a splice

If S is the leaf support of a finite proof tree rooted at an output state, then every table in Q(S) satisfies that same proof tree. In a successful Gap-MCSP cover, all such tables have circuit complexity at most s2. Therefore

    Q(S) subset SIZE(s2), and ell(S), u(S) are in SIZE(s2). (3)

Now fix an accepting proof with an occurrence of state i. Let K be the support outside that occurrence, and let C be the support of any finite replacement proof rooted at i. Substitution is valid at the proof-tree level. Its accepted interval, if nonempty, is

    Q(K union C)=Q(K) intersect Q(C) subset SIZE(s2).

In particular, every compatible context/replacement pair obeys

    1_{P(K)} OR 1_{P(C)} is in SIZE(s2),
    1_{[N] minus Z(K)} AND 1_{[N] minus Z(C)} is in SIZE(s2). (4)

This is a support-level OR/AND law: compatible reuse forces both canonical extreme completions of the mixed support to be low. It is not a closure of SIZE(s2) under arbitrary OR or AND; the pairs are restricted to context/subproof pairs generated by one sound grammar.

## 3. Simultaneous substitution law

Suppose a fixed accepting proof has pairwise-disjoint marked occurrences v_1,...,v_t, and at occurrence j we substitute any finite proof C_j rooted at the same state as v_j. Let K be the support outside all marked subtrees. The resulting tree is valid and its support is

    S=K union C_1 union ... union C_t.

If S is consistent, then its entire interval lies in SIZE(s2), and its endpoints are

    ell(S)=ell(K) OR bigvee_j ell(C_j),
    u(S)=u(K) AND bigwedge_j u(C_j).                         (5)

So every compatible tuple of simultaneous replacements must have a low positive-support join and a low negative-support meet. This statement needs no block-isolation hypothesis; block isolation is still needed to guarantee that particular target hybrid tables satisfy the mixed support.

At an empty-carrier output rule i, the side grammar in C-227 gives the corresponding local product: for every E-side support a and every H-side support b, their support union `a ∪ b` is a valid output proof support. Every nonempty intersection Q(a) intersect Q(b) is therefore contained in SIZE(s2), with endpoints ell(a) OR ell(b) and u(a) AND u(b). The Cartesian product is over all side derivations, including pairs never used together by the original low-table proofs.

## 4. How this changes the splice attack

The C-227/C-230 attack asks for a consistent mixed support with more than kappa_square(s2)=Theta(s2 log s2) free coordinates. Equations (3)-(5) expose a second possible contradiction: it is enough to force either canonical endpoint itself to have circuit complexity above s2. This can bypass the need to exceed the maximum safe-subcube dimension. On the repeated-block family of C-234, those endpoints are concrete ownership hybrids: at a coordinate pinned by either side they take the common or sole pinned value, while coordinates pinned by neither side are set to 0 in the lower endpoint and 1 in the upper endpoint. A theorem forcing a high-complexity ownership endpoint from reuse would directly contradict soundness.

This reformulation does not by itself force such an endpoint. A low endpoint pair is already guaranteed by soundness for every actual splice. The missing global statement remains: show that a small q-state grammar covering all of SIZE(s1) must generate compatible cross-products whose OR/AND endpoint cannot both stay in SIZE(s2), or prove that avoiding such profiles costs superlinear q.

## 5. Hostile checks

- C-228 isolated-anchor promise: every sound accepting interval is a singleton in its independent set W. Its two endpoints coincide with an already accepted anchor. The endpoint law permits safe sharing and creates no forbidden hybrid.
- C-234 diagonal subpromise: the O(N) equality-of-blocks separator accepts only repeated-block tables. Every nonempty accepting interval for that separator is a singleton diagonal table, so compatible substitutions remain harmless. This confirms that the endpoint law does not incorrectly infer block isolation or a full-promise lower bound.
- C-230 safe-cube calibration: full interval inclusion in (3) implies the existing free-dimension ceiling. The endpoint formulation adds an algebraic object to attack, not a stronger numerical bound by itself; certificate width remains tight to constants.

## 6. Literature screen: expansion and local substitutions

Austrin and Risse's CCC 2023 paper proves SoS proof-degree/size lower bounds for certifying circuit lower bounds and gives analogous results for minimum monotone-circuit size on monotone Boolean slice functions. Its stated general method uses CSP incidence-graph expansion together with substitutions of variables by local Boolean functions ([paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2023.31)). This is a useful construction motif for the present block-mixing question: replace repeated independent blocks by overlapping local views controlled by an expander, then ask whether closure reuse must respect one common latent function.

There is no transfer yet. Their theorem concerns SoS refutation complexity for circuit-size assertions (and a monotone slice variant), whereas C-75 measures a positive cyclic recognizer on truth-table inputs. To use the motif here, one would still need an explicit embedding of the low/high truth-table promise into a hard monotone slice function, a proof that local substitutions preserve the low anchors and make no-instances high, and a compiler from q-state fusion closure to the relevant monotone circuit/proof model with controlled loss. The embedding is the main obstacle; without it, this is inspiration rather than evidence for a fusion lower bound.

## Status and exact next obligation

Proved: support/cube identity, endpoint OR/AND formulas, soundness for every proof interval, and the simultaneous-substitution product law. These are exact consequences of the C-227 finite-proof grammar.

Not proved: any endpoint is high for a small closure; a superlinear native fusion lower bound; a near-linear upper cover for the full promise; or P != NP.

The next useful theorem is a global endpoint-mixing dichotomy: for the block-replicated low family, either a q-state grammar has enough independent context/replacement profiles to realize a robustly high ownership endpoint, or its nonlocal overlap/fingerprint structure has a superlinear state cost. C-228 and the diagonal equality cover must remain counterchecks.
