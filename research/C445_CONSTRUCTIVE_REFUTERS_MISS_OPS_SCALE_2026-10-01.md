# C-445 — Constructive refuters do not reach the OPS scale

**Date:** 1 October 2026  
**Status:** targeted 2026 literature check and threshold audit. It sharpens the certificate obstruction but yields no new lower bound or separator.

## 1. The scale mismatch

Write the truth-table input length as `N=2^n`. For every fixed `β>0`, the High cutoff is

```text
s2 = N^β = 2^(βn),
```

and `2^(βn)/(cn) → ∞` for every fixed constant `c`. Thus a refuter that handles circuits of size `O(n)` is exponentially below the size `s2` that must be excluded. Raising such a refuter to `s2` for an explicit family would establish an exponential-in-`n` unrestricted circuit lower bound, far beyond the linear lower bounds supplied by the current gate-elimination methods.

## 2. What the recent results actually give

- Carmosino, Dang, and Jackman make selected gate-elimination proofs constructive. Their affine refuter finds an affine subspace on which each challenged circuit is constant; for an explicit affine disperser this gives a `P^NP` refuter against size `3n-o(n)` circuits. Their XOR refuter reaches `3(n-1)` DeMorgan gates. The paper explicitly says that known explicit unrestricted lower bounds remain linear in `n`. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23958)
- Their multiplexer result gives a constructive DeMorgan lower bound `2N+log N-2` where the MUX has `N=2^n` data inputs. This is also linear in the number of data inputs, not `N^(1+ε)`. [Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23958v1)
- The 2026 convergent term-graph paper gives a modular proof of the classical XOR lower bound `3(n-1)` in the DeMorgan basis; its abstract does not claim a superlinear general-circuit bound. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17942)
- The September 2026 local-decision-tree paper gives near-maximal bounds for an *oblivious restricted model* and explains conditional routes from sufficiently strong local-decision-tree bounds to improved general-circuit bounds. Those conditional implications are not an ordinary-circuit lower bound for GapMCSP. [ECCC TR26-195](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/195/)

These results strengthen the ability to find a mismatch for a particular fixed function and challenged circuit. They do not produce a short certificate that a table is High against **every** size-`s2` circuit. The latter is the universal part of `∀d ∃a: Eval(d,a)≠T[a]`; a per-description selector still needs a proof of its total correctness. Nor do the cited lower bounds supply a useful polynomial-size QBF refutation family for C-444's `W` at this threshold.

## 3. Counterconstruction audit

The requested patterns defeat local-count-to-gate-cost arguments, but they do not decide the full promise.

| Pattern | Cheapest relevant shared computation | What it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| Address parity `f(a)=⊕_i a_i` | `O(n)` gates (linear in the address length) | Parity of table-address bits, sensitivity, or many changed table entries cannot alone force superlinear computation. |
| Repeated table blocks / equality copies | A shared equality tree checks all `N` input-bit pairings in `O(N)` gates; if the repeated block is generated by a simple address rule, evaluate that rule directly | Charging one fresh gate per repeated anchor or block. This is a subpromise recognizer only. |
| `O(N)` sparse parity-check incidences | Compute each check with a shared XOR tree and AND the check outputs, costing `O(N)` gates when total incidences are `O(N)` | Counting local constraints or mismatch sites as separate computational work. |
| Simple global block relations | Reuse a common block value/address decoder and check all `O(N)` relations in `O(N)` gates | Arguments that count correlated blocks without proving a cost for their shared readout. |

These are counterexamples to the proposed charging mechanisms, not to a superlinear lower bound for an arbitrary full-promise separator. In particular, an `O(N)` recognizer for one structured subpromise says nothing about all Low and all High tables.

## 4. Full-promise construction attempt

The direct exact separator still enumerates all size-`s1` circuit descriptions, checks each against all `N` coordinates, and ORs the results. This gives the known total-gate upper `O(N·2^{O(N^β)})` (absorbing `log N` into the constant in the exponent for fixed `β`). Replacing each explicit mismatch list by a shared refuter does not remove the outer universal quantifier over descriptions. No near-linear full-promise separator was constructed here.

## 5. Conclusion and next mechanism

The strongest result in this cycle is a scope correction: constructive counterexample search for each circuit is not a certificate for the universal High predicate, and current explicit unrestricted lower bounds are at scale `O(n)`, whereas `s2=2^(βn)`. This also makes C-444's proof-certified subfamily nontriviality obligation a serious separate lower-bound task; feasible interpolation does not solve it.

Retire gate-elimination refuters as a direct route to the OPS exponent unless a new argument compresses the **proof of totality over all descriptions** at the `s2` cutoff. Retain them as tests for any proposed witness-selection mechanism. The next research turn will follow the user's requested project-wide first-principles audit, then choose a mechanism only after reconciling this quantifier gap with the repeated-block, sparse-check, and full-promise counterchecks.

**Frontier unchanged:** ordinary separator lower bound `N-O(N^β log N)-1` plus C-406's additive refinement; the OPS target `N^(1+ε)` remains open for one fixed `ε>0` and all sufficiently small fixed `β>0`; exact full-promise upper `O(N·2^{O(N^β)})`; native `ρ≥N-o(N)` remains separate. No P-vs-NP result follows.
